# Construct a Fantasy Map From Geographic Framework to Finished Art

## Objective
Carry a pictorial fantasy map from scope decisions through a revisable geographic framework, resolved map features, protected framing and information areas, typography, and an inked, painted, or digital finish without letting local detail outrun the dependencies that make the whole map coherent and readable.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Define the map contract before drawing.** Decide the intended geographic scope, physical/output size, monochrome versus color finish, and any references that will guide the map. Apply `PAT_plan_map_land_water_framework_from_geographic_intent` before committing local geography.
2. **Establish a light whole-map framework.** Block the major land and water masses first, then mark broad terrain zones and placeholder locations for settlements, rivers, borders, corners, and other required infrastructure. Keep this pass cheap enough to revise; it is a placement framework, not a detail pass.
3. **Pass the geographic-framework gate.** The large land/water arrangement and major feature zones must coexist at the intended scale before detailed coastline or symbol work begins. Preserve any already-known perimeter or layout reservations while judging that framework. If the large arrangement does not work, revise it rather than polishing a local feature.
4. **Resolve the primary geographic structure before its decorative echoes.** Apply `PAT_refine_coastline_from_broad_framework_to_clear_boundary`, then develop major terrain, settlement structure, and the river network with their accepted owners. River mouths must connect cleanly through the coast before decorative shoreline reinforcement is treated as authoritative.
5. **Add dependent water-edge decoration only after the true boundary reads.** Apply `PAT_reinforce_coastline_with_simplified_offset_shoreline_echoes` to stable coasts and large lakes. Interrupt or stop subordinate water decoration where reserved border or information-panel space requires clear separation; do not let decorative echoes overrule the true coast or protected layout areas.
6. **Resolve framing, information, and orientation infrastructure before filling every open area.** Establish the neatline/border and corner treatment, reserve and develop the legend or other information panels, and place the compass rose while the composition still has room to accommodate them. Treat these areas as protected space for later terrain, water marks, routes, and labels.
7. **Fill secondary geographic systems around the resolved structure.** Construct forests and other repeated terrain fields, then add route classes, political boundaries, repeatable icons, heraldry, and other minor map information as needed. Preserve readable separation from rivers, settlements, panels, and other already-authoritative features rather than covering them with filler detail.
8. **Resolve typography after the feature field is mature.** Apply `PAT_build_map_label_hierarchy_by_semantic_class` and the relevant point, linear, or area-label owners. Reserve or clear subordinate terrain where needed so labels read cleanly; do not force text through finished feature marks merely because those marks were drawn first.
9. **Pass the pre-finish gate.** Before final inking or paint, the map should already work as a complete drawing: primary geography, terrain systems, settlements, routes/boundaries, framing, information panels, orientation, iconography, and required typography must all be present or deliberately omitted. Repair unresolved collisions or missing information while the drawing is still cheap to change.
10. **Choose the finish branch deliberately.** For an ink-only finish, establish primary boundaries and container outlines with the strongest line hierarchy, then major descriptive forms, then fine local detail; stop when the finished inks and any restrained wash already satisfy the map. For traditional color, final inking may be skipped when paint is intended to carry the finish. For digital work, apply `PAT_separate_digital_art_elements_into_ordered_editable_layers` so linework, color, typography, and other elements remain independently adjustable while needed.
11. **Develop color from broad relationships toward local finish when color is required.** Establish large color fields and shared shading/value relationships before resolving small isolated features. Then develop rivers, settlements, forests, border elements, heraldry, compass, typography, and other terrain with the relevant owners, using `PAT_control_color_layering_with_transparency_opacity_and_ground` when layered color is needed. Keep broad structure ahead of minor detailing while developing the required feature colors and finishes.
12. **Finish by reconciling the whole map.** Add the remaining required details, complete information panels and final typography/color, repair local shading, highlights, or linework, and check that late additions do not obscure geography, labels, orientation, or hierarchy. Finish only after those systems read together as one coherent artifact.

## Notes
This procedure coordinates existing cartographic owners instead of replacing them. Its durable order is dependency-based: framework before detail, true geographic boundaries before decorative reinforcement, protected panels before dense filler, mature features before final typography, and a complete drawing before irreversible finish. Medium-specific finish may branch, but each branch must preserve the resolved map structure.
