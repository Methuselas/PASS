# Construct Compass Rose With Clear Directional Hierarchy and Orientation Cues

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a map needs a decorative orientation symbol
**THEN** construct the compass rose around a shared center, keep the cardinal directions distinct, give north stronger emphasis when using a north-led hierarchy, and preserve unambiguous directional cues even after ornament is added

## Do
- Establish a common center so the directional points read as one system.
- Keep the cardinal directions visually separable from one another.
- When using a conventional north-led treatment, make north more prominent than the other directions.
- Add direction letters or another clear cue that makes orientation explicit.
- Use the center as an optional decorative field only when the ornament leaves the directional structure readable.

## Don't
- Do not turn the compass into a decorative star whose orientation cannot be read.
- Do not let center ornament obscure the directional points or labels.
- Do not assume one fixed point count, exact geometry, or lettering style is required for every map.
- Do not give every directional point identical emphasis when the design intends north to lead.

## Checklist
- All directional points share one coherent center.
- Cardinal directions remain distinguishable at the intended map size.
- North has visibly stronger emphasis when a north-led hierarchy is used.
- Directional labels or cues make orientation unambiguous.
- Ornament supports rather than replaces the compass function.

## Notes
A compass rose can carry substantial decoration, but its first job remains orientation. Geometry, point count, center ornament, and lettering style can change as long as the directional system stays legible.
