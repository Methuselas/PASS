# Construct Pictorial Chasm From Opposing Cliff Rims and Deepening Interior

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a pictorial map needs a chasm or fissure to read as an opening cut below the surrounding ground plane
**THEN** establish two irregular opposing rim contours that broadly correspond without mechanically mirroring, expose descending interior faces from projecting parts of the rims, and deepen the visible interior value as light access decreases so the opening reads as depth rather than a flat enclosed stripe

## Do
- Establish one rim with a sweeping, predominantly lateral irregular contour when the map uses an oblique or isometric terrain view.
- Add the opposing rim so it follows the same broad course while changing locally in spacing and shape; where the fissure ends are visible, let the opening narrow into the surrounding ground rather than ending as a blunt parallel band.
- Use projecting points and bends along the visible rim as anchors for descending or returning face lines so the viewer can read the cut surface between the ground plane and the deeper opening.
- Keep the major rim-and-face structure readable before adding rock texture.
- Let layered rock marks broadly follow the cliff course, and use shorter vertical or broken marks to clarify face direction without competing with the main rims.
- Taper sparse cracks or broken terrain marks outward from the chasm when they are needed to integrate the cut into the surrounding ground.
- When tone or color is used, keep the exposed faces distinct from the ground and progressively deepen the interior toward the least accessible depth; add stronger local darks beneath ledges or overlaps only where the geometry supports them.
- Keep highlights restrained and concentrated on exposed upper surfaces or edges so they clarify the rim without making ordinary rock appear highly reflective.

## Don't
- Do not draw the chasm as two evenly spaced parallel lines with no descending faces or interior depth cue; that collapses the feature into a flat stripe or border.
- Do not mirror every bend of one rim exactly on the other; preserve the shared course while allowing natural irregularity in width and edge shape.
- Do not begin with sediment bands, hatching, cracks, or color before the paired rims and visible cliff faces establish the opening.
- Do not let surface-detail marks ignore the direction of the cliff faces they describe.
- Do not keep the entire visible interior at one even value when the intended read depends on increasing depth and decreasing light access.
- Do not extend integration cracks into the surrounding terrain as uniform untapered bands that compete with the main fissure.

## Checklist
- The two rim contours read as opposite boundaries of one cut through the ground rather than as unrelated lines.
- Projecting rim points and descending or returning lines make at least part of the exposed cliff wall legible.
- Where a terminal is visible, the opening narrows into the surrounding terrain instead of ending as a blunt ribbon.
- The deepest visible interior is darker than the more exposed upper faces when tone or color is present.
- Rock bands, hatching, and cracks reinforce the established face directions instead of replacing the large structure.
- The chasm remains recognizable when small texture, decorative cracks, and color accents are ignored.

## Notes
A chasm reads first as a paired-edge depth structure. The opposing rims establish the footprint of the opening, while descending face lines convert that footprint from a flat symbol into a cut below the ground plane. Layer-following rock marks can then describe the exposed surfaces. When rendering is added, a darker interior toward less accessible depth strengthens the sense that the fissure continues below what the viewer can see; it should reinforce the construction rather than substitute for it.
