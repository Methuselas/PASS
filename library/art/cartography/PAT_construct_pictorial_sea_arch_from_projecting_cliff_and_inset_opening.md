# Construct Pictorial Sea Arch From Projecting Cliff and Inset Opening

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a pictorial coastal cliff needs a recognizable natural arch rather than an unbroken cliff face
**THEN** build the cliff as a projecting top edge with descending faces, cut a rounded opening through the projection, and add the visible interior return of the opening so the rock reads as having thickness
**ELSE** keep the cliff face continuous when no pierced coastal feature is intended

## Do
- Establish the upper cliff edge with a sweeping directional stroke, then drop visible faces from the projections so the coast reads as raised terrain rather than a flat boundary.
- Close the lower cliff faces where they meet the water and let their lower contours broadly follow the structure established above.
- Place the opening inside a projecting part of the cliff and shape it as a simple rounded negative space before adding small surface detail.
- Add an offset inner contour on the visible inside wall of the opening so the viewer can see the rock thickness through the arch.
- Use vertical or layered rock marks to clarify the direction of the cliff faces after the large top, face, opening, and thickness relationships already read.
- Add nearby rocks or sparse terrain marks only after the main cliff-and-opening silhouette is clear enough to survive without them.

## Don't
- Do not draw the arch as a single outline cut from a flat coastline; without the cliff faces and inner return it will read like a paper hole instead of a solid rock opening.
- Do not detail sediment, cracks, grass, or small rocks before the projecting cliff mass and opening are structurally readable.
- Do not let face-detail marks ignore the orientation of the cliff surfaces they are meant to describe.
- Do not force the opening into a perfect circle or one fixed cliff silhouette when the surrounding landform calls for another readable shape.

## Checklist
- The coastline feature reads as raised cliff terrain with an identifiable top edge and descending face.
- The opening is visibly contained inside a projecting rock mass rather than merely interrupting a flat contour.
- At least one visible inner edge makes the thickness of the arch legible.
- Surface marks support the cliff orientation without overpowering the major silhouette and opening.
- The feature remains recognizable when minor rocks, grass, texture, and color are ignored.

## Notes
The critical depth cue is the interior return of the opening. A rounded negative shape establishes the passage, but the second contour on the visible inside wall turns that passage into a hole through thick rock. Cliff-face marks can then reinforce the surface direction, while water and foreground rocks may provide additional overlap cues without becoming part of the core construction.
