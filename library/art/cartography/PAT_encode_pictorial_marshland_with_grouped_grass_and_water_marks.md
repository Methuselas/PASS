# Encode Pictorial Marshland With Grouped Grass and Water Marks

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a marsh or wetland needs to read as an areal terrain field rather than as isolated point symbols
**THEN** repeat small grass-tuft groups across the intended wetland extent, anchor them with short horizontal ground or water marks, and add lighter line or sparse-dot texture until the marks read collectively as marshland

## Do
- Establish the intended wetland extent before filling it with detail.
- Distribute grass groups across the area rather than treating each tuft as a separate location.
- Use short horizontal marks to suggest wet ground or water around the vegetation.
- Keep supplemental texture lighter than the main grass cues.

## Don't
- Do not space the marks so sparsely that they read as unrelated grass icons.
- Do not fill the area so densely that the terrain becomes an unreadable dark patch.
- Do not let secondary dots or water marks compete with the primary wetland cues.

## Checklist
- The field reads as one wetland area at map scale.
- Grass groups are distributed across the intended extent.
- Horizontal wet-ground or water cues support the terrain reading.
- Supplemental texture remains subordinate.

## Notes
Marshland is an area texture, not a special-location point glyph. The repeated marks should accumulate into a terrain field whose extent remains readable.
