# Integrate Corner Flourish With Neatline and Map Field

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a map corner needs a decorative extension that connects it more strongly to the neatline or reaches into the map field
**THEN** anchor the flourish to the corner/frame system, establish a clear major-to-minor directional hierarchy, and resolve any interrupted or overlapped framing so the embellishment still reads as part of the perimeter

## Do
- Begin from the existing corner or border rather than placing the flourish as an unrelated interior illustration.
- Establish one or more major extensions before adding smaller connected elements.
- Keep subordinate branches, clusters, or decorative marks visibly secondary to the major directions.
- Allow the flourish to run along the neatline or project inward when that strengthens the corner-to-frame transition.
- Restore or deliberately resolve any neatline area cleared for the flourish.

## Don't
- Do not let the flourish detach visually from the corner or border that motivates it.
- Do not fill the extension with equal-weight detail until its directional structure disappears.
- Do not assume a botanical motif, symmetry, or one fixed shape is required.
- Do not let ornament cover map information that needs to remain easier to read.

## Checklist
- The flourish has an obvious attachment to the corner/frame system.
- Major extensions establish the silhouette before smaller detail is read.
- Any neatline interruption or overlap looks intentional.
- The flourish can project into the map field without becoming an unrelated illustration.
- Important map information remains legible beside the ornament.

## Notes
The variant `VAR_expand_corner_flourish_with_longer_multiarm_illustrative_structure` increases reach and complexity through longer or multiple major arms, then develops smaller offshoots and illustrative elements beneath them. It is useful when a richer perimeter treatment is wanted, but the frame attachment and hierarchy must remain clear.
