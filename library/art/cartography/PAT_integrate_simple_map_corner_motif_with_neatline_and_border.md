# Integrate Map Corner Motif With Neatline and Border

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a map border needs an illustrated corner motif
**THEN** clear enough local frame area to construct the corner from simple geometry, add only the detail the treatment needs, and redraw the interrupted neatline and border elements so the motif becomes part of the perimeter

## Do
- Open enough space at the corner that the motif can be constructed cleanly.
- Start from simple geometric masses before adding decorative detail.
- Keep repeated internal marks controlled and visually even when the design depends on repetition.
- Reconnect the neatline and border to the finished motif so the frame terminates into and resumes from the corner cleanly.
- Match complexity to the surrounding map treatment instead of assuming every corner needs the same level of ornament.

## Don't
- Do not paste a decorative object over the corner without resolving how the border meets it.
- Do not begin with uncontrolled detail when a simple scaffold can establish the silhouette first.
- Do not require every corner to be identical, mirrored, or symmetrical unless the chosen design system calls for it.
- Do not let corner ornament overpower the map field it frames.

## Checklist
- The corner motif has a clear underlying structure.
- The neatline and border connect cleanly into the motif.
- Detail remains subordinate to the corner silhouette and the larger map.
- Any repeated internal marks are deliberate rather than irregular accidents.
- The corner reads as part of the frame rather than a floating illustration.

## Notes
The variant `VAR_build_complex_map_corner_from_geometric_scaffold_and_layered_organic_detail` increases sophistication by layering nested, interwoven, or organic detail over a simple scaffold. Use it when the map treatment benefits from a richer corner, while keeping the border connection and overall silhouette readable.
