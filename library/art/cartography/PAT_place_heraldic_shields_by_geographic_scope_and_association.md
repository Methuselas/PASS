# Place Heraldic Shields By Geographic Scope and Association

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a separate heraldic shield must be placed on a map and its intended geographic scope must be clear
**THEN** match placement and scale to what the shield represents, using close association for a specific city and larger, more isolated placement for a broader regional sphere

## Do
- Keep a city shield close enough to its city icon for immediate one-to-one association.
- Leave enough clearance that the city symbol and shield remain separately readable.
- Place a regional shield larger and in relatively open space so it reads as an area-level marker.
- Keep regional shields away from nearby cities when proximity would imply ownership by one point.
- Use local topographic overlap only when it preserves the intended association without obscuring more important information.

## Don't
- Do not place a city shield so far away that another feature becomes a plausible owner.
- Do not crowd the city symbol with the shield.
- Do not place a regional shield directly beside one city when it is meant to represent a broader sphere.
- Do not require an explicit boundary line when the heraldic marker already communicates the intended area-level influence.

## Checklist
- City shields have one obvious associated city.
- Regional shields read as broader-area markers rather than point attachments.
- Scale and spacing reinforce geographic scope.
- Important underlying map information remains readable.

## Notes
Separate heraldic markers can communicate political or cultural influence at different geographic scopes. Proximity establishes point association; larger isolated placement shifts the read toward regional influence.
