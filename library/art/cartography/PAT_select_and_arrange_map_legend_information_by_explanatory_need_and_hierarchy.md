# Select and Arrange Map Legend Information by Explanatory Need and Hierarchy

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a map legend needs its internal information selected and arranged
**THEN** use the map title as the primary anchor, include symbol, boundary, scale, or other explanations according to what the map actually needs clarified, and rough the full information layout before final lettering so repeated entries align and spacing stays controlled

## Do
- Establish the title first in the legend hierarchy.
- Include icon or boundary definitions when the map uses symbols or conventions that need explanation.
- Reserve a coherent area for a scale when the map includes one.
- Rough in all major information groups before committing final typography.
- Align repeated symbol entries and keep their spacing visibly consistent.
- Leave enough separation between information groups that they do not crowd one another.

## Don't
- Do not add legend categories merely because another map might use them.
- Do not finalize lettering before checking whether all required information fits the panel.
- Do not let repeated symbol entries wander in alignment or spacing.
- Do not make supporting definitions compete with the title for primary emphasis.

## Checklist
- The title is the clearest information anchor.
- Every included legend item explains something the map actually uses.
- Major information groups fit without crowding.
- Repeated symbol entries align consistently.
- Any scale area is reserved as a coherent part of the legend layout.

## Notes
The legend should explain the map that actually exists, not collect every possible category. Solve title, supporting definitions, symbol rows, and any scale area together before committing final typography.
