# Site Fortifications By Defensive Role And Protected Feature

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a map needs a castle, keep, or other defensive strongpoint placed before its icon is committed
**THEN** place the strongpoint where terrain advantage or proximity to an important city, major river or bridge crossing, valuable coastal route, or political boundary supports the defensive job the fortification is meant to perform, and keep the site light and revisable until that relationship works

## Do
- Use dominating high ground when observation or territorial defense is the fortification's job.
- Place major strongpoints near important cities, river or bridge crossings, valuable coastal routes, or political boundaries when those are the features being protected or controlled.
- Mark candidate sites lightly enough to move them while judging defensive logic.
- Choose the location from the strongpoint's job rather than from a generic preference for castles on hills.

## Don't
- Do not place every castle on high ground regardless of what it protects.
- Do not treat defensive placement as identical to settlement placement around resources and trade.
- Do not commit detailed fortification artwork before the site makes strategic sense in the map.
- Do not generalize beyond the defensive relationships the map actually needs.

## Checklist
- The fortification's location has a clear defensive relationship to terrain or a protected feature.
- High ground is used when it provides the intended observation or territorial advantage.
- Strongpoints near routes, crossings, cities, coasts, or boundaries have an identifiable protective or control role.
- The site remains revisable until that role reads in the map.

## Notes
Fortification placement is a different decision from ordinary settlement siting. The strongpoint earns its location through defensive leverage: terrain advantage, protection, observation, or control of something strategically valuable.
