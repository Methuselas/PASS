# Use Bespoke Pictorial Landmarks for Story-Significant Locations

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a place has individual narrative or setting identity that matters more than classifying it as another standard map feature
**THEN** replace the generic repeated symbol with a bespoke pictorial landmark built from a clear primary form and a few identifying cues, integrating it with surrounding terrain when that context is part of the location’s identity

## Do
- Establish a primary silhouette that reads before small detail.
- Choose only a few cues that communicate what makes the place distinctive.
- Clear or reserve local generic terrain texture when an embedded landmark needs room to read.
- Reconnect surrounding terrain after the landmark is stable so it feels part of the geography.
- Add shading or color only after the landmark identity is clear.

## Don't
- Do not turn every ordinary location into a bespoke illustration.
- Do not bury the landmark under the same generic terrain texture it is meant to interrupt.
- Do not depend on many tiny details for the location identity.

## Checklist
- The landmark is distinguishable from repeated category symbols.
- Its primary form reads at map scale.
- A small set of cues explains why the place is distinctive.
- Embedded landmarks feel integrated with surrounding terrain rather than pasted over it.

## Notes
Bespoke landmarks are for individually significant places. Repeatable categories should use a reusable glyph family instead of redrawing a unique illustration for every instance.
