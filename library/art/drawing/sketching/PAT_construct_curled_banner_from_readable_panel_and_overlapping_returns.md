# Construct Curled Banner From Readable Panel and Overlapping Returns

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a banner, scroll, ribbon, or similar flexible sheet needs a broad readable front face with one or more curled ends
**THEN** establish the readable front panel first, use one long edge to set the sheet's main curve, turn the end regions back through self-overlapping returns, angle their side contours with the curl, and close the opposite long edge with a broadly corresponding curve before adding only enough inner return lines to show which parts face forward, turn away, or roll back
**ELSE** keep the sheet simpler when a flat panel or uncurled strip communicates the function more clearly.

## Do
- Reserve the usable front-face area before adding curls so ornament or surface content does not consume the space the object is meant to carry.
- Let one long edge establish the dominant sweep, then make the opposite edge correspond closely enough that the central face reads as one flexible sheet rather than unrelated contours.
- At a curled end, let the sheet overlap itself and use the visible return to separate the front face from the turned-away face.
- Angle descending or side contours in the direction implied by the curl; let them taper, flare, or change direction as needed to preserve the turn in depth.
- Add a short interior return, doubled edge, or comparable thickness cue only where it clarifies the rolled edge or face change.
- Keep any lettering, emblem, or surface design registered to the visible sheet: when a curl passes in front of that content, let the nearer fold occlude it.
- Add tears, texture, material marks, color, and lighting only after the panel, overlaps, and curled returns already read in line or simple value.

## Don't
- Do not build the curls before reserving enough coherent front-facing area for the banner's intended information or decorative role.
- Do not make every end contour hang straight down when the sheet is visibly curling sideways or backward; that contradicts the turn and flattens the form.
- Do not mirror every fold exactly; preserve related edge behavior without making the flexible sheet mechanically symmetrical.
- Do not draw both overlapping faces as one uninterrupted contour when a return should pass behind or in front of another part of the sheet.
- Do not let text or ornament float above a fold that should physically cover it.
- Do not use shading or texture to rescue a curl whose overlap and return geometry are unclear.

## Checklist
- The central front panel remains readable before rendering or surface detail.
- Each curled end has an understandable near/far relationship created by overlap and return geometry.
- Side contours support the direction of the curl rather than contradicting it with arbitrary verticals.
- The two long edges describe one coherent flexible sheet.
- Any visible thickness cue clarifies a turn without becoming an unnecessary parallel outline everywhere.
- Surface content follows the visible face and is hidden where nearer folds occlude it.
- The object still reads when color, texture, cast shadow, and highlight are removed.

## Notes
A curled banner is easiest to control when its information-bearing face and its fold topology are solved before surface finish. The central panel establishes the usable field; a dominant long-edge curve sets the sheet's sweep; overlapping end returns explain which portions turn forward, backward, or around themselves. The opposite edge should correspond to the same sheet rather than imitate the first line mechanically. Small thickness or return cues can clarify the roll, but overlap carries most of the depth information. Lettering and ornament are surface content, so they inherit the same occlusion order as the sheet that carries them.
