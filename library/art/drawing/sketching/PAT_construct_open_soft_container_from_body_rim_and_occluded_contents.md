# Construct Open Soft Container From Body, Rim, and Occluded Contents

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an open flexible container and its visible contents must read clearly from a simplified, top-down, or high-angle view
**THEN** establish the outer body mass and inner mouth as separate simple shapes, deform that scaffold into a soft irregular silhouette, give the opening a readable rim or thickness, and build the visible contents as overlapping partial forms before adding folds, texture, or lighting
**ELSE** use a simpler closed-container construction when the opening and contents do not need to be shown.

## Do
- Set placement and scale with the outer body mass and the opening before committing surface detail.
- Treat the opening as a distinct interior shape so the viewer can tell where the container turns inward.
- Break the clean scaffold with controlled bulges, compressions, or uneven contour only after the large shapes are placed, so softness does not destroy the footprint.
- Use nested, broken, or interrupted contours around the mouth when they help communicate a folded rim, thickness, or layered fabric.
- Build visible contents gradually with partial contours and overlap; let nearer contents interrupt farther ones so the pile reads as depth rather than a flat pattern.
- Add folds, stipple, weave, grain, color variation, cast shadow, and highlights only after the body, rim, and content stack already read.

## Don't
- Do not begin with wrinkles, weave, stipple, or individual contents before the outer mass and opening are established.
- Do not preserve perfectly smooth concentric guides when the container is meant to read as flexible and filled.
- Do not draw every repeated content form as a complete isolated symbol; complete contours everywhere flatten the pile and weaken occlusion.
- Do not let soft-contour irregularity change the intended footprint, opening direction, or container function by accident.
- Do not use texture or shading to compensate for an opening whose rim and depth relationship are unclear.

## Checklist
- The outer body and open mouth are distinguishable before texture or color is considered.
- The rim or mouth communicates enough thickness or folding to read as an opening rather than a symbol printed on the surface.
- The silhouette feels flexible without losing the intended placement and scale.
- Visible contents form a coherent near-to-far stack through partial contours and overlap.
- Surface texture and lighting reinforce an already readable construction instead of carrying it alone.
- The object remains legible at its intended final viewing scale.

## Notes
Open soft containers combine two problems that should be solved together: a deformable body with an inward-turning mouth, and a visible stack of contents inside that opening. Simple nested masses establish the footprint and aperture first; controlled contour irregularity supplies softness; overlap then organizes the contents into depth. Material marks and lighting belong after those relationships work, because neither burlap texture nor shading can repair a flat opening or an unordered pile.
