# Construct Hand Lettering With Temporary Top, Middle, and Baseline Guides

## Pattern Rule
**IF** hand-lettered text needs controlled letter height and consistent upper- and lowercase proportions
**THEN** establish light temporary baseline and top-height guides, add a middle guide when mixed-case proportions need control, construct the lettering inside that scaffold, then remove the guides after the text is resolved

## Do
- Draw the baseline lightly enough that it can be removed after lettering.
- Add a parallel top guide to establish the intended overall letter height.
- Add a middle guide when upper- and lowercase relationships need a consistent reference.
- Keep the letters inside the intended guide relationships while preserving the chosen letterform style.
- Remove the construction guides after the lettering is complete enough to stand without them.

## Don't
- Do not let temporary guides become part of the finished lettering unless they are intentionally retained as design elements.
- Do not change letter height arbitrarily from character to character when the text is meant to read as one line.
- Do not make the guides so heavy that removing them damages or muddies the finished lettering.
- Do not treat the middle guide as mandatory when the lettering system does not need mixed-case proportion control.

## Checklist
- The baseline and top guide establish a consistent text height.
- A middle guide is present only when it helps control the chosen letter proportions.
- The letters remain coherent with the intended guide relationships.
- The temporary construction lines can be removed without losing the lettering structure.
- The finished text reads as a consistent line rather than a sequence of independently sized characters.

## Notes
Temporary height guides separate proportion control from letterform styling. The scaffold is useful wherever hand lettering must stay consistent across a line: solve the baseline and height relationships first, use a middle reference only when the case system needs it, then remove the construction once the lettering carries those proportions itself.
