# Choose Publication Vehicle from Content Change and Reader Use

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the same subject could be delivered through more than one kind of publication
**THEN** choose the publication vehicle by how the information changes over time, how often the audience needs it, whether its parts arrive serially or as a coordinated system, and how readers are expected to obtain and use it
**ELSE** keep the prescribed vehicle and use those factors to test whether its structure actually fits the job.

## Do
- Determine whether the content is continually changing, periodically updated, finite but expandable, or a coordinated set of related messages.
- Match rapidly or repeatedly changing information to a periodical form when recurring delivery genuinely serves the audience; newspapers, magazines, and newsletters are common examples rather than mandatory templates.
- Match finite or componentized information that must be encountered as a related family to a systematic form when that better fits the job; catalogs and coordinated brochure systems are common examples.
- Use the audience's required update frequency as a design input. Daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, occasional, and event-triggered information place different demands on the vehicle.
- Account for expected access and use: subscription, membership distribution, newsstand discovery, reference use, record keeping, or decision support can change which vehicle is practical and intelligible.
- Treat category conventions as starting hypotheses. Keep them when they make the information easier to understand, but change them when another structure fits the actual content and audience more clearly.
- Resolve the vehicle before detailed physical-format decisions, then pass the chosen form to `PAT_choose_publication_format_from_content_physical_feel_and_production_constraints` for size, binding, page count, portability, and manufacturing decisions.

## Don't
- Do not choose a periodical merely because the subject resembles a magazine or newspaper category if the audience does not need recurring updates.
- Do not assume one subject implies one inevitable publication type; the same subject can require different vehicles for different audiences, cadences, and uses.
- Do not let competitor convention substitute for examining how the present information changes and how readers will use it.
- Do not postpone the vehicle decision until after detailed layout when it determines content grouping, recurrence, navigation, and production expectations.
- Do not confuse publication vehicle with trim size or binding. The vehicle answers how the communication is delivered as a publication; the physical format answers how that vehicle is embodied.

## Checklist
- The content's pattern of change over time is understood.
- The audience's information cadence is explicit.
- The publication's parts are known to be serial, grouped as a system, or otherwise structured in a way the chosen vehicle can support.
- Expected access and use are compatible with the vehicle.
- The chosen vehicle was selected for communication fit rather than inherited automatically from a category convention.
- Physical format remains a separate downstream decision.

## Notes
Publication type is not only a label applied after the content is designed. A periodical is appropriate when new material must repeatedly build on an ongoing subject for readers who expect updates. A coordinated system is appropriate when related components carry different submessages within one larger communication. Other vehicles can solve the same subject in different ways. The durable decision is to match the form of delivery to content change, reader need, and use before committing to the object's trim, binding, and detailed page architecture.
