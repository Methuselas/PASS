# Derive Handbound Cover and Spine Geometry from the Actual Page Block

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a handmade cover, spine, or hinge must fit a finished set of folded or stacked pages
**THEN** build and measure the actual page block, then derive the cover dimensions, spine width, hinge or groove allowance, and attachment positions from that physical stack before cutting the final cover parts
**ELSE** preserve an already proven cover geometry only when the paper, page count, and binding thickness have not materially changed.

## Do
- Fold, gather, or stack the real interior stock in its intended page count before fixing the final spine width.
- Measure the assembled thickness rather than relying only on nominal paper specifications or an earlier digital estimate.
- Transfer the measured block into the cover construction so the spine can contain the pages without squeezing them or leaving excessive slack.
- Leave the opening clearance required by the chosen cover and hinge construction instead of placing rigid boards directly against one another.
- Square and align the page block and cover parts before punching, stitching, or gluing so the binding does not lock in accumulated registration error.
- Rebuild or remeasure the block after a material or page-count change that alters thickness.

## Don't
- Do not guess final spine width before the actual interior stock and page count are known.
- Do not reuse cover dimensions from a thinner or thicker dummy without checking the new page block.
- Do not eliminate hinge or groove clearance merely to make the closed cover look tighter.
- Do not punch or glue misaligned parts and expect stitching or pressure to correct the geometry afterward.

## Checklist
- The measured page block uses the intended stock and final or representative page count.
- Spine width reflects the actual assembled thickness.
- Cover and hinge geometry permits the book to open without binding against itself.
- Cover parts and page block are square and registered before permanent fastening.
- Any later material or pagination change that affects thickness triggers a new measurement.

## Notes
The physical page block is the reliable dimensional authority for a hand-bound cover. Folded signatures and stacked sheets accumulate real thickness that abstract page counts do not fully predict. Measuring the assembled block and deriving the spine and hinge geometry from it keeps the finished cover fitted to the object that actually exists rather than to an estimate made earlier in the design process.
