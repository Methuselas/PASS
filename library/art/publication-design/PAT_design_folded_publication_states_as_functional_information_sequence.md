# Design Folded Publication States as a Functional Information Sequence

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a printed sheet changes what the reader can see or do as it is folded and unfolded
**THEN** map the closed, intermediate, and fully open states, assign each state a useful information or handling role, and organize panels and fold-defined bands so the physical transformation clarifies sequence, reference, or action
**ELSE** treat the sheet as an ordinary flat page when folding does not materially change access to the information.

## Do
- Draw the complete front-and-back panel map and mark fold lines, panel orientation, and what remains visible at each meaningful state before finishing the layout.
- Decide what the closed state must accomplish, such as identification, containment, mailing, or a concise first message, and what the fully opened state must accomplish, such as overview, instruction, comparison, or ordering.
- Use fold-defined panels or bands to group information whose relationship becomes clearer through progressive opening or side-by-side reference.
- Choose the number and direction of folds from the information capacity and navigation behavior the content requires; add or remove partitions when the current fold scheme either fragments reference or cannot expose enough material at once.
- Keep recurring directional cues, headings, or other navigation devices consistent enough that the reader can tell where to continue after each fold is opened.
- Build full-size physical mockups early and rearrange real content on them when necessary; judge information flow by opening, closing, turning, and referring back rather than from the flat screen layout alone.
- Apply `PAT_protect_critical_content_from_physical_production_boundaries` so fold creases do not damage small type, precision diagrams, or other vulnerable information.

## Don't
- Do not compose the flat sheet first and impose folds afterward without checking which panels will actually be visible together.
- Do not multiply folds merely for novelty when the reader must repeatedly reopen the object to compare closely related information.
- Do not let panel orientation force the reader to rotate the piece unpredictably unless that rotation serves a deliberate use.
- Do not split a sentence, diagram, price, label, or other tightly coupled information across a crease when the separation makes reference slower or ambiguous.
- Do not judge a foldout only in its fully opened state when the audience will first encounter and handle it folded.

## Checklist
- Closed, intermediate, and open states have been mapped at final size.
- Each meaningful state has a clear role rather than being an accidental by-product of the fold scheme.
- Related information is visible together when the reader needs to compare or act on it.
- Headings and navigation cues make the unfolding path understandable.
- Fold lines and panel orientations do not damage or confuse critical information.
- A physical mockup confirms that opening, reference, refolding, and handling work as intended.

## Notes
A foldout is both a surface and a small physical interface. Folding controls which information is available together, how the reader moves from one group to another, and whether the object can change roles as it opens. A compact folded form may identify or contain the publication while the opened sheet becomes an overview, instruction set, catalog, or other working field. The useful design move is to let the information structure determine the folds and then verify the result by handling a full-size mockup, rather than treating the creases as decoration imposed on a finished flat layout.
