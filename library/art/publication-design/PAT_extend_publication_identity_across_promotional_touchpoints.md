# Extend Publication Identity Across Promotional Touchpoints

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a publication will also appear on websites, press material, event pieces, or other promotional surfaces
**THEN** carry recognizable visual elements from the publication into those touchpoints while adapting the hierarchy and composition to each format
**ELSE** avoid building a separate promotional identity system for a publication that will only exist in one controlled context.

## Do
- Identify a small set of transferable identity elements from the publication, such as type treatment, imagery, recurring pattern, color relationships, or another distinctive visual cue.
- Reuse those elements selectively so a viewer can connect the promotion back to the publication without requiring an exact copy of the cover.
- Adapt scale, crop, hierarchy, and information density to the new surface instead of forcing a book-shaped composition into every format.
- Keep a clean cover image and other representative publication imagery available for contexts that need direct recognition of the product.
- Check that the adapted materials still express the same visual character even when their dimensions and information needs differ.

## Don't
- Do not redesign every promotional piece as an unrelated visual world.
- Do not paste the complete cover unchanged into a format where its hierarchy becomes too small or crowded to work.
- Do not introduce new display treatments that overpower or contradict the publication's established identity without a deliberate reason.
- Do not mistake repetition for consistency; preserve recognizable relationships while letting each touchpoint solve its own layout problem.

## Checklist
- Promotional pieces can be visually associated with the publication at a glance.
- The identity survives changes in size, crop, and format.
- Each touchpoint has a hierarchy suited to its own viewing conditions.
- Reused elements remain readable and functional rather than merely decorative.
- The publication and its supporting materials feel like one project without becoming identical copies.

## Notes
A publication's cover often becomes the strongest portable image of the project, but the useful design move is broader than copying that cover everywhere. Reusing selected visual elements across web and promotional material creates recognition while allowing each surface to be composed for its own constraints.
