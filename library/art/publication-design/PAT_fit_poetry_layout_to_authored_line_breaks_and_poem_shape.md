# Fit Poetry Layout to Authored Line Breaks and Poem Shape

## Pattern Rule
**IF** poetry is being typeset and authored line breaks or overall poem shape materially affect the reading
**THEN** choose typeface, type size, page measure, and placement by testing the longest lines and longest poems, preserving their intended lineation while giving each poem a deliberate visual position on the page
**ELSE** use the publication's established text system when the poem imposes no special line-length or placement demand.

## Do
- Identify the longest line and longest poem before fixing type size and text measure.
- Choose a typeface and size that let important authored lines fit comfortably without routine accidental wrapping.
- Preserve authored line and stanza breaks that are important to the poem's reading.
- Judge the poem as a whole text shape after setting it; position that shape deliberately rather than centering it mechanically by default.
- Test the system against the collection's longest lines and longest poems rather than judging it only from shorter examples.

## Don't
- Do not let a convenient column width routinely force new line breaks into lines the layout could accommodate by choosing a more suitable type size or measure.
- Do not choose type only from a specimen page if the longest line or largest poem fails in the actual format.
- Do not assume mathematical centering is the only way to place a poem; judge the visual weight of the complete text shape on the page.

## Checklist
- The longest important lines fit comfortably without routine unintended wrapping.
- Authored line and stanza breaks remain visible as intended structures rather than artifacts of the layout.
- Typeface and size work for the collection's demanding examples, not only average ones.
- Each poem's placement feels deliberate when judged as a whole visual mass.
- The layout preserves the poem's reading logic without creating cramped or excessively fragile pages.

## Notes
Poetry makes lineation unusually visible as both language and shape. A publication system therefore has to be tested against the poems that place the strongest demands on line length and page depth. Optical placement can help irregular poems sit with authority on the page, but the goal is not a universal centering formula; it is to let the authored structure survive typesetting while the resulting text shape belongs deliberately to the page.
