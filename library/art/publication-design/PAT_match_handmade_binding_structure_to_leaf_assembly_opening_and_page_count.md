# Match Handmade Binding Structure to Leaf Assembly, Opening, and Page Count

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a handmade publication can be assembled with more than one folded, stitched, or stab-bound structure
**THEN** choose the binding from how the leaves are formed, how many pages must be held, how freely the book must open, the flexibility of the paper, the desired durability, and the labor the edition can sustain
**ELSE** verify that the available binding remains compatible with the page block and intended handling before committing the physical design.

## Do
- Use a folded or accordion structure when connected folded panels serve the sequence and a quick construction with little or no stitching is desirable.
- Use a single sewn signature for a modest group of nested folded sheets when a simple pamphlet structure provides enough capacity and stability.
- Move to multiple signatures when page count exceeds a comfortable single signature, and allow the spine to widen enough for the separate sections to open without being crushed together.
- Consider stab binding when the interior consists of loose single sheets or compatible French folds, and reserve enough edge area for the fastening because the stitched margin does not open like a center fold.
- Match paper flexibility to the chosen structure: folds need stock that will crease and stay folded, while stab-bound leaves need enough flexibility to turn near the stitched edge.
- Choose soft versus hard cover behavior from the use: a flexible cover opens more easily, while a hard cover adds protection, formality, and longevity but may require a hinge or other opening allowance.
- Count repeated hand operations across the intended edition before choosing a structure whose assembly burden scales poorly.

## Don't
- Do not choose a binding because its exposed stitch or cover looks attractive if the page block cannot open or turn comfortably in it.
- Do not force a large page count into one overstuffed signature when multiple sections would handle the thickness more cleanly.
- Do not use stiff interior stock in a structure that depends on repeated folding or flexing near the binding.
- Do not assume a hard cover is automatically superior when its stiffness makes the chosen binding harder to use.
- Do not ignore the fastening margin of a stab-bound book when laying out text or imagery near that edge.

## Checklist
- The leaf structure matches the selected binding: connected folds, nested signatures, multiple signatures, or single sheets are intentional rather than accidental.
- The binding can hold the planned page count without excessive bulk or distortion.
- Pages turn and the book opens as freely as the intended use requires.
- Interior and cover stocks flex, fold, and protect in ways compatible with the chosen construction.
- The required hand labor is plausible for the planned edition size.

## Notes
Handmade binding is a structural design choice, not merely a finishing technique. Accordion folds, single- and multiple-signature pamphlets, and stab bindings accept different page architectures and behave differently when opened. Cover stiffness, paper flexibility, page count, and labor all interact with that choice. Select the construction from those physical relationships first, then use its visible character as part of the publication's design.
