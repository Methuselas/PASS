# Optically Finish Typeset Text Beyond Font Defaults

## Pattern Rule
**IF** publication text has been composed and its broad typographic system is stable
**THEN** inspect punctuation, symbols, emphasis styles, line breaks, paragraph endings, and neighboring columns optically, then make local corrections where software defaults create collisions, uneven rhythm, misleading spacing, or distracting breaks
**ELSE** fix the body-text system first when the defects recur broadly enough to indicate a measure, size, leading, or alignment problem rather than a local finishing problem.

## Do
- Treat automatic kerning and spacing as a starting point. Correct conspicuous pairs or punctuation relationships when their visible counterspaces break the surrounding rhythm.
- Check parentheses, brackets, quotation marks, commas, colons, semicolons, dashes, slashes, and other symbols for collisions, excessive gaps, awkward baseline position, or spacing that visually disconnects them from the phrase they serve.
- Use the punctuation mark appropriate to the text's language and editorial convention, then adjust its optical fit rather than substituting one mark merely because its default spacing looks easier.
- Evaluate italics, bold weights, small caps, bullets, and other inserted styles against the surrounding text. Correct size, spacing, weight, or baseline when the insertion appears to shrink, swell, crowd, or jump out unintentionally.
- Review hyphenated line endings as a sequence. Avoid weak fragments and distracting runs of hyphens, but correct recurring excess by recalibrating measure or type size instead of hand-forcing every line.
- Prevent a single isolated final word from creating a visibly weak paragraph ending when a small non-destructive adjustment can avoid it, and avoid beginning a new column with the stranded final line of the preceding paragraph.
- In adjacent columns, look for aligned paragraph gaps or other horizontal channels that pull the eye across the columns and interrupt the intended reading order.
- Use local optical corrections sparingly enough that later reflow remains manageable and the underlying paragraph system stays intact.

## Don't
- Do not assume a font or layout application's defaults have solved every punctuation and symbol relationship.
- Do not cure rivers or bad line endings with extreme tracking that creates noticeably dark or loose lines.
- Do not make dozens of local fixes when the same defect keeps returning; repair the governing paragraph system instead.
- Do not rewrite authoritative wording solely to make typography easier unless editorial authority for that change has been explicitly granted.
- Do not chase absolute freedom from hyphens, widows, or other line-ending artifacts at the cost of worse spacing and less stable text.

## Checklist
- Punctuation and symbols sit naturally within the surrounding rhythm.
- Emphasis styles and small inserted forms belong optically to the text around them.
- Line endings do not produce conspicuous weak fragments, repeated hyphens, or stranded paragraph remnants.
- Adjacent columns do not form accidental horizontal channels that redirect reading.
- Local corrections are exceptional rather than compensating for a broken body-text standard.
- The corrected text still reflows and proofs predictably.

## Notes
Fine typesetting is the stage where nominally correct text becomes optically coherent. Fonts and layout software encode many useful defaults, but they cannot anticipate every letter pair, punctuation collision, symbol, line break, or column relationship. The durable distinction is between a local optical defect and a systemic defect: correct the first locally; return the second to the paragraph specification, alignment, or page architecture that owns it.
