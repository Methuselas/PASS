# Structure Publication Sequence for Orientation, Content, and Reference

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a bound or multipage publication contains orientation material, primary content, and later reference material
**THEN** assign each section to a deliberate beginning, middle, or ending role so readers encounter identity and navigation before the main content and supporting reference material after it
**ELSE** keep a simpler sequence when the publication does not need all three roles.

## Do
- Identify which opening elements actually help readers identify, enter, or navigate the publication before styling them.
- Keep the primary content in a continuous middle sequence unless a deliberate interruption improves use.
- Place appendices, glossaries, indexes, production notes, or comparable reference material after the main content when they support rather than initiate the reading experience.
- Use a table of contents when readers need an overview or direct access to separated sections; treat it as both navigation and an early statement of what the publication contains.
- Place folios where they can be found consistently and preserve the left-page/even, right-page/odd relationship of a conventionally bound sequence.
- When folded signatures, saddle stitching, or a meaningful centerfold constrain placement, make a pagination diagram that shows the full page sequence, spread pairs, signature boundaries, and centerfold before finalizing content positions; use it to test how inserting, deleting, or shifting material changes page count and flow.
- Omit ceremonial or luxurious matter when it does not serve the publication's actual use; a utilitarian manual does not need every conventional book part.

## Don't
- Do not include front or back matter merely because another book has it.
- Do not hide navigation after the material it is supposed to help readers find.
- Do not let decorative opening pages consume space needed for orientation in a utility-first publication.
- Do not rearrange conventional page sequence casually when the change makes location and navigation harder to predict.

## Checklist
- Readers can identify the publication and reach its main content without unnecessary detours.
- Navigation appears before it is needed.
- Main content and reference material have distinct sequence roles.
- Optional matter is present because it serves the publication, not because it is customary.
- Folios and page parity remain consistent with the bound sequence.
- When physical pagination matters, the full page/signature map has been checked for the consequences of inserts, deletions, and centerfold placement.

## Notes
A book is not only a stack of designed pages; it is an ordered reading object. Front matter establishes identity and orientation, the middle carries the primary content, and back matter supports later reference or production information. The exact inventory is flexible: omit pieces that do not help the intended use rather than treating a conventional anatomy as mandatory. When binding imposes signature or centerfold structure, sequence planning benefits from a pagination diagram rather than mental arithmetic alone. Mapping page parity, spread order, signature boundaries, and content placement together exposes whether a seemingly local insertion or deletion will disrupt the publication's larger flow or page-count structure.
