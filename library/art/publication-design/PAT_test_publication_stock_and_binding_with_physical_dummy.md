# Test Publication Stock and Binding with a Physical Dummy

## Pattern Rule
**IF** paper stock, cover stock, binding, or page-count choices will materially affect how a publication feels, opens, closes, or holds together
**THEN** compare real material samples and build or obtain a blank bound dummy at the intended size and approximate page count before finalizing the physical specification
**ELSE** confirm that the chosen materials and construction have no unresolved physical behavior that a flat layout preview cannot reveal.

## Do
- Compare candidate papers in the hand for weight, surface, color, and stiffness rather than selecting them from names or screen images alone.
- Assemble the dummy with the intended trim size, approximate page count, text stock, cover stock, and binding class so thickness and handling are represented together.
- Open and close the dummy repeatedly to see whether it lies open, springs shut, flops excessively, or loses usable area near the binding.
- Compare the cover's weight and stiffness against the interior so the object feels intentionally constructed rather than accidentally mismatched.
- Feed problems revealed by the dummy back into margins, gutter allowance, stock choice, page count, and binding before final production files are locked.

## Don't
- Do not approve a physical specification from a flat digital spread when binding behavior is still unknown.
- Do not select stock by appearance alone when fold, stiffness, surface, or opening behavior matters.
- Do not assume the cover and interior stocks will feel balanced because their numeric specifications look plausible.
- Do not build a dummy at a substantially different thickness or format and then treat its opening behavior as representative of the final object.

## Checklist
- The dummy matches the intended trim size and approximate thickness closely enough to test handling.
- Candidate paper and cover stocks have been handled as physical samples.
- Opening behavior, gutter loss, stiffness, cover/interior relationship, and overall feel have been checked.
- Any physical problem found in the dummy has been resolved in the publication specification or page architecture.

## Notes
A paper dummy turns abstract production specifications into a holdable test object. Stock weight, surface, cover-to-interior contrast, thickness, and binding behavior interact in ways that a screen preview cannot show. Use the dummy before the layout becomes expensive to change, especially when the publication must lie open, carry dense information near the gutter, or achieve a particular physical character.
