# Treat Visible Binding Materials as Functional Visual Language

## Pattern Rule
**IF** stitching, thread, cover paper, cloth, board, folds, or other construction elements remain visible in the finished publication
**THEN** choose their color, material, spacing, and finish as part of the publication's visual language while preserving the mechanical job each element must perform
**ELSE** keep hidden construction visually subordinate and judge it primarily by function and durability.

## Do
- Treat exposed stitches as both fastening and visible rhythm; choose thread color and placement to support the publication's character without weakening the seam.
- Use cover papers, cloth, photographs, sketches, or other suitable surface materials deliberately when the cover construction leaves them visible.
- Let the material contrast between cover, spine, thread, and pages reinforce the intended tone when that contrast can be controlled coherently.
- Resolve the binding's structural requirements first, then make expressive choices inside those limits rather than sacrificing opening behavior or durability for decoration.
- Inspect the closed book, open book, fore edge, and spine because visible construction contributes to the object from more than one viewing angle.

## Don't
- Do not treat thread color, exposed folds, or cover material as afterthoughts when they are conspicuous parts of the finished object.
- Do not choose decorative materials that are too stiff, fragile, thick, or poorly adhered for the structural role they must perform.
- Do not add ornamental stitching that confuses or interferes with the fastening pattern.
- Do not assume a visually elaborate cover compensates for a binding that opens badly or fails under handling.

## Checklist
- Visible construction elements support a coherent visual character rather than appearing accidental.
- Every expressive binding element still performs its structural role reliably.
- Cover and thread materials are physically compatible with the chosen binding.
- Spine, fore edge, and open-spread views have all been checked as parts of the publication's appearance.

## Notes
In many handmade books, construction is not hidden: stitches, folds, hinges, spine, and cover materials remain visible and therefore participate in the design. A colored thread can become an accent and a stab-binding pattern can become a graphic rhythm, but those choices remain constrained by the job of holding the publication together and allowing it to be used. Treat craftsmanship and visual identity as one coordinated problem rather than decorating the binding after it has been solved mechanically.
