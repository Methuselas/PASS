# Unify Multi-Contributor Publication with Shared Constraints

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a publication combines visually distinct contributions from multiple makers
**THEN** establish a small set of shared production constraints that touch every contribution, such as common format and a limited color or ink system, while leaving image, texture, lettering, and local treatment open enough for contributors to retain distinct voices
**ELSE** use the simpler visual system appropriate to a single-maker publication.

## Do
- Choose a few constraints that every contributor can actually work inside before production accelerates.
- Use format and a deliberately bounded palette or ink set as strong unifying controls when the publication still needs wide local visual freedom.
- Let contributors vary imagery, pattern, texture, type, hand lettering, and other local treatment when that variation is part of the publication's collective character.
- Circulate or cross-review layouts when a communal result matters so contributors can respond to the developing whole rather than working as isolated islands.
- Assemble and inspect the pages together before finalizing; unity has to survive the complete publication, not just individual contributions.

## Don't
- Do not demand one identical drawing or lettering style from every contributor merely to make the publication feel coherent.
- Do not leave every production variable open when the result needs to read as one publication.
- Do not assume a shared topic alone will visually unify work made by many people.
- Do not judge contributors only in isolation when adjacent pages can make the combined sequence feel fragmented or unexpectedly coherent.

## Checklist
- Every contribution obeys the agreed shared production constraints.
- The shared constraints are few enough that contributors still have meaningful room for individual treatment.
- Format and color or ink behavior remain consistent enough to bind the publication together.
- Cross-review or assembled-sequence inspection has exposed obvious visual drift between contributions.
- The finished publication reads as a collective object without erasing the differences between its makers.

## Notes
A collaborative publication can gain coherence from common production limits rather than from stylistic uniformity. A fixed format and restricted color system can provide a visible common ground while contributors range widely across imagery, pattern, texture, typography, and hand lettering. When layouts circulate among contributors, the developing publication can acquire a communal visual character instead of reading as a stack of unrelated pages.
