# Build Stake Weight Through Player Investment Before Threatening It

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure expects players to care about the possible loss of an NPC, community, location, cause, relationship, promise, duty, or objective
**THEN** create opportunities for the players to know it, use it, depend on it, choose for it, sacrifice for it, or otherwise invest in it before the threat asks that investment to carry emotional weight
**ELSE** treat the threatened element as an unproven stake rather than assuming fictional scale or authorial importance will make it matter.

## Do
- Count fictional investment created through play: repeated interaction, chosen obligations, shared victories, sacrifices, dependence, protection, promises, and consequences can all create stake weight.
- Give an important stake enough table exposure for players to form their own relationship to it before the design relies on its loss or danger.
- Separate **threat magnitude** from **stake weight**. A kingdom, planet, or civilization can be a weaker stake than one person, place, or promise the players have actually come to value.
- Let players affect the threatened element before the crisis when possible; agency creates stronger investment than exposition alone.
- Carry earlier relationships, discoveries, duties, and choices forward when the stake becomes endangered so the threat feels like a consequence of accumulated play rather than a newly announced importance label.
- When a high-investment fictional stake is damaged or destroyed, make the aftermath acknowledge the changed relationship and state rather than treating the loss as disposable scenery.
- Permit different groups to assign different weight to the same prepared element; design for opportunities to invest, not a mandatory emotional response.

## Don't
- Assume increasing the fictional scale of a threat automatically increases its emotional force.
- Threaten an NPC, community, place, faction, or cause as if the players must care merely because the adventure labels it important.
- Substitute a long backstory or dramatic description for opportunities to interact, choose, rely, sacrifice, or form obligations.
- Introduce a supposedly beloved element immediately before threatening it and expect the same weight as a relationship accumulated through play.
- Predetermine the emotion the players must feel after a loss; preserve the consequences and let their response remain theirs.

## Checklist
- Each important threatened element has at least one concrete path by which players can invest in it before the threat peaks.
- Threat magnitude and accumulated player investment are evaluated separately.
- The design does not rely solely on exposition, title, status, or fictional scale to establish importance.
- Earlier interaction or choice can materially resurface when the stake is threatened.
- The aftermath of a high-investment loss makes the changed state perceptible.
- The adventure allows players to care differently without invalidating the scenario.

## Notes
Stake weight is accumulated state, not a label the author can simply attach. A smaller loss can carry more force than an abstract catastrophe when prior play has given the players reasons to care. The *Volturnus* sequence demonstrates the structure over multiple adventures: players meet societies, solve problems, discover the Sathar threat, and accumulate relationships before the Battle of Volturnus places those relationships at risk. Louis V. Jameson's fate likewise pays off the original search mission because the later discovery reaches back to an earlier objective. The reusable structure is **expose -> interact -> invest -> threaten -> acknowledge consequence**.
