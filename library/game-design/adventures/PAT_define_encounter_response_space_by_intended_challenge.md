# Define Encounter Response Space by Intended Challenge

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an encounter presents a problem the players must answer
**THEN** decide which response classes the challenge is intended to support and make those routes legible enough to choose, without adding fake alternatives or forcing openness when a narrow challenge is the point
**ELSE** do not impose a multiple-solution quota merely to make the encounter appear agency-rich.

## Do
- Name the intended response classes before writing detailed encounter content: fight, negotiate, evade, investigate, retreat, manipulate the environment, spend resources, seek allies, surrender, solve, accept a cost, or another route that fits the fiction.
- Support more than one route when the encounter's purpose benefits from strategic or role-based choice.
- Allow a deliberately narrow response space when the challenge is specifically a duel, escape, answer, race, survival test, or other constrained problem.
- Give players enough information to understand that a route exists before penalizing them for not discovering it, unless uncertainty about available routes is itself part of the challenge.
- Let specialist capabilities open better, cheaper, safer, or broader routes without making the specialist the only permission key for routine progress unless that exclusivity is intentional.
- Test whether nominally different solutions actually create different costs, risks, information, state changes, or future consequences.

## Don't
- Add several nominal solutions solely to make an encounter look open-ended when all branches produce the same practical result.
- Require combat when the fiction and objective support another intended route.
- Add an automatic bypass to every puzzle or focused challenge merely because failure would otherwise matter; decide explicitly whether failure should stop, redirect, or complicate play.
- Treat one exact answer as mandatory when the actual challenge is broader and several responses would satisfy its purpose.
- Hide every noncombat route so thoroughly that only author knowledge reveals it exists, unless discovery itself is the intended challenge.

## Checklist
- The encounter's intended response classes are explicit.
- Any narrow response space is deliberate and justified by the challenge's purpose.
- Multiple routes, when offered, differ in meaningful cost, risk, information, consequence, or required capability.
- Players have a reasonable basis for recognizing available routes at the point a choice matters.
- Specialist routes provide meaningful leverage without accidentally becoming compulsory keys unless exclusivity is intended.
- No artificial solution-count quota has been imposed.

## Notes
Agency does not require every encounter to support fighting, talking, sneaking, and puzzle solving simultaneously. It requires the response space to match the intended challenge and for advertised choices to be real. A duel may honestly require victory; a collapsing structure may honestly require escape. Open-ended problems benefit from multiple consequential interfaces, while focused challenges benefit from clear constraints. The design error is not narrowness but pretending that a narrow challenge is broad, or pretending that several cosmetic branches are meaningful alternatives.
