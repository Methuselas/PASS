# Delegate Bounded Encounter-Space Authorship to Players

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an encounter would benefit from player ownership and emergent spatial variety without surrendering the scenario's load-bearing structure
**THEN** fix the shared scale, central anchor, connection rules, and plot-critical elements, give players bounded regions or components to create, have them declare what they made, assemble the contributions into one shared space, and treat the resulting geometry as binding terrain for play
**ELSE** author the encounter space centrally when secrecy, exact calibration, or discovery of the map itself is more important than co-creation.

## Do
- Keep a stable scenario anchor under facilitator control, such as a centerpiece, objective, plotline, or other element that gives the assembled space a common purpose.
- Give each player a bounded construction surface, component budget, theme, scale, or connection constraint so contributions can be combined without requiring one player to design the whole encounter.
- Use a shared spatial grammar so independently created sections connect and can be adjudicated consistently once assembled.
- Ask each player to describe or label the important features of their contribution before play depends on them, so the table shares the same understanding of what the constructed space represents.
- Preserve player-created geometry as real scenario state once it is accepted; let elevation, cover, routes, line of sight, hazards, and objectives interact with what was actually built or drawn.
- Let the facilitator add threats, clues, resources, or consequences around the contributed terrain after assembly, while keeping the declared meaning of the players' contributions intact.
- Leave unused or lightly specified regions open when the group has fewer contributors than available construction space rather than forcing filler for symmetry.

## Don't
- Ask players for unrestricted world creation when the encounter still depends on unstated scale, connection, or structural assumptions.
- Outsource the only required objective, sole progress-critical clue, or another load-bearing element to a contribution that may not appear.
- Secretly rewrite a player's declared structure after assembly merely to preserve a prepared tactic or surprise.
- Treat player construction as decorative if the resulting routes, heights, barriers, or other spatial facts never affect play.
- Use this method when discovering an unknown environment is itself the main challenge and pre-authoring the space would erase that uncertainty.

## Checklist
- The facilitator can name which scenario elements remain fixed before player construction begins.
- Each player contribution is bounded by enough shared constraints to connect with the rest of the encounter space.
- Important features have an agreed meaning before their geometry is used for adjudication.
- At least one consequential decision can change because of player-created terrain, position, route, elevation, cover, or access.
- The assembled space remains playable even if one contribution is sparse, eccentric, or absent.
- Player-authored spatial facts are not retroactively invalidated merely because they complicate facilitator plans.

## Notes
Player co-creation becomes mechanically meaningful when the group does more than decorate a prepared map. A bounded construction method can preserve a facilitator-authored premise and central objective while transferring local spatial authorship to the players. The shared scale and connection grammar make separately created pieces compatible; the declaration step makes their meaning explicit; and treating the assembled result as real terrain turns authorship into later tactical and exploratory consequences.

This differs from general downstream construction guidance because the authored material is created as part of the encounter's immediate setup and then consumed directly by the same table. It also differs from ordinary map notation: the key decision is who creates the local spatial state, not merely how that state is represented once it exists.
