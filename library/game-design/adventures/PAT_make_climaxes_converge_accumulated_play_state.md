# Make Climaxes Converge Accumulated Play State

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure reaches a climactic problem
**THEN** let the alliances, preparation, discoveries, losses, promises, resources, relationships, and other state accumulated through prior play materially change the climax's conditions, objectives, and available solutions
**ELSE** do not reset the adventure to a predetermined final challenge that ignores what the players already accomplished.

## Do
- Identify the pieces of prior play that could reasonably alter the climax before fixing its final form.
- Let earned allies, information, preparation, positioning, equipment, favors, injuries, losses, and changed relationships alter opposition, access, objectives, leverage, or risk.
- Allow excellent prior play to make the climax easier when that is the honest consequence of preparation.
- Allow prior play to change the climax's objective, open negotiation, create an escape, divide opposition, bypass a confrontation, or transform what success means.
- When the climax contains a dilemma, author the competing values, duties, loyalties, information, and consequences while leaving the protagonist's chosen answer to the player.
- Prepare the facilitator to recognize unexpected but earned convergence when players bring forward state the outline did not anticipate.

## Don't
- Restore an imagined target difficulty by silently cancelling earned advantages.
- Require the climax to be the hardest encounter, a combat, or a surprise merely because conventional dramatic structure expects those properties.
- Treat prior relationships or preparation as flavor once the final scene begins if they would logically affect the problem.
- Predetermine the morally correct answer to a climactic dilemma and use the adventure to force it.

## Checklist
- Important accumulated state has a defined way to affect the climax when relevant.
- Earned preparation can change difficulty, access, leverage, objectives, or available solutions rather than merely adding narration.
- The climax can change shape when earlier play materially changes its premises.
- Any central dilemma presents genuinely competing pressures without prescribing the protagonist's response.
- The facilitator has permission to honor an earned bypass, negotiation, or easier victory instead of restoring the planned challenge.

## Notes
A climax is strongest when it cashes in what the table has already made important. The final problem then feels like a consequence of play rather than a separate set piece waiting behind the adventure. The *Volturnus* sequence demonstrates the principle at campaign scale: relationships, preparation, and prior discoveries matter when the conflict expands into planetary defense. The reusable structure is **build -> invest -> converge**: establish what matters, let players invest in it, then allow those investments to reshape the pressure at the point of convergence.
