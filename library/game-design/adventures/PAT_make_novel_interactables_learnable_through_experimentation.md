# Make Novel Interactables Learnable Through Experimentation

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure includes an unfamiliar object, environment, phenomenon, or local rule intended to reward experimentation
**THEN** give it consistent discoverable behavior, informative feedback, consequential risks, and reusable affordances so players can learn how it works and exploit that knowledge creatively
**ELSE** resolve ordinary objects through the game's established interaction rules.

## Do
- Establish a stable underlying rule for the interactable before play, even when the characters do not initially know it.
- Provide observable outputs from reasonable tests so players can form and revise hypotheses instead of guessing a secret answer with no feedback.
- Let different inputs, tools, positions, materials, timings, or combinations reveal different parts of the behavior when that creates meaningful experimentation.
- Allow discovered behavior to become a tool for solving later problems, altering routes, manipulating threats, obtaining rewards, or creating unexpected plans.
- Make risks proportionate to the evidence available; telegraph irreversible or extreme consequences strongly enough for experimentation to remain a decision rather than a trapdoor guess.
- Keep the mechanism accessible through player reasoning rather than requiring one class, skill, spell, or predetermined solution unless that restriction is the point of the feature.
- Preserve surprising consequences that follow from the established rule even when players use the feature in ways the designer did not anticipate.
- Record enough operating state for the facilitator to adjudicate repeated use consistently.

## Don't
- Change the feature's behavior ad hoc to block a clever use that follows its previously established rules.
- Make experimentation a disguised single-answer riddle where every unanticipated approach simply fails.
- Hide all useful feedback until after the players have already committed to an irreversible high-consequence result.
- Require specialized character power for every meaningful interaction when the feature is supposed to create broadly accessible problem solving.
- Introduce a bizarre object whose only function is one predetermined scene and whose discovered rules never matter again.
- Explain the mechanism completely before players have a chance to investigate when discovery is part of the intended play.

## Checklist
- The interactable has a consistent underlying behavior the facilitator can state clearly.
- Reasonable experiments produce feedback that can support a better next decision.
- Severe consequences have an information basis appropriate to their stakes.
- More than one character concept can meaningfully investigate or exploit the feature when broad problem solving is intended.
- Learned behavior can matter beyond the first successful interaction.
- Unanticipated uses can be adjudicated from the established rule rather than designer intent alone.
- Repeated use does not require the facilitator to reinvent the feature's behavior each time.

## Notes
Novel interactables temporarily reset system mastery. Players may know the optimal use of familiar attacks and skills, but a strange local mechanism creates a new problem whose rules must be discovered through play. The design value comes from consistency and reuse: once players learn the rule, the feature changes from mystery into an affordance they can deliberately combine with the rest of the environment. That transition rewards observation and experimentation rather than answer guessing.
