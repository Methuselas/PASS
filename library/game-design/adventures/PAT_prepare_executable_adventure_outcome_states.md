# Prepare Executable Adventure Outcome States

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure can end or hand off into later play from materially different states
**THEN** prepare recognizable resolution, continuation, and redirected-failure outcomes with trigger conditions and consequences that preserve what was actually won or lost
**ELSE** do not force every conclusion into one intended victory or terminal failure state.

## Do
- Prepare at least one valid stopping state even when the larger campaign remains unresolved.
- Distinguish a **resolution ending** that closes the current arc, a **continuation ending** that closes the current arc while handing momentum into later play, and a **redirected-failure state** in which the immediate objective is genuinely lost but play continues under materially changed conditions when those forms are relevant.
- Preserve the truth of failure when play continues after it. Defeat can lead to retreat, occupation, resistance, rescue, recovery, or a new objective without being relabeled as victory.
- Give important outcome states recognizable trigger conditions so the facilitator can tell which prepared consequence applies.
- Provide enough material to combine prepared states when play produces a mixed or unexpected result.
- Let continuation hooks preserve rather than negate closure: later threats can grow from the ending without retroactively erasing the accomplishment that made the current arc satisfying.
- After the climax, communicate changed state and consequences efficiently so the table understands what its actions meant before play releases or moves on.

## Don't
- Force every ending into victory or terminal failure when the established state supports a meaningful continuation or redirected failure.
- Convert a genuine loss into secret success merely because the campaign needs to continue.
- Use a sequel reveal that makes the players' just-completed victory meaningless solely to manufacture a larger threat.
- Leave the facilitator with only one scripted ending when the preceding play can legitimately produce materially different states.
- Turn falling action into a long monologue that communicates consequences while giving players no meaningful participation or clear stopping point.

## Checklist
- At least one valid stopping state exists for the current adventure.
- Additional resolution, continuation, or redirected-failure states are prepared when the adventure can plausibly reach them.
- Each important outcome has an identifiable trigger or state condition.
- Prepared consequences preserve what the players actually won, lost, spent, protected, or changed.
- Mixed outcomes can be combined or adapted without pretending the adventure reached a state it did not.
- Continuation material opens later play without invalidating closure earned in the current arc.
- The aftermath communicates the changed state clearly enough that the table knows what the result means.

## Notes
An adventure does not need a single canonical ending to have closure. It needs executable stopping states that tell the facilitator what the current play state means. A lost battle may honestly become retreat or resistance; a local victory may close the present arc while opening a later threat. The key is to preserve causality. In the *Volturnus* sequence, a conventional military setback can redirect play into guerrilla resistance until outside help changes the strategic situation. Continuing the campaign does not require pretending the lost battle was actually won.
