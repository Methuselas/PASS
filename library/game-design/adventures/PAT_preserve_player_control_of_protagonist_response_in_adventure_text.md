# Preserve Player Control of Protagonist Response in Adventure Text

## Pattern Rule
**IF** adventure text describes what happens to or around a player-controlled protagonist
**THEN** state external circumstances, sensory information, NPC behavior, and mechanically imposed states while leaving voluntary actions, judgments, emotions, and chosen reactions to the player
**ELSE** do not use narration to make protagonist choices on the player's behalf.

## Do
- Describe what the protagonist can perceive, what other characters do, and what external conditions change.
- State involuntary effects when an explicit rule actually imposes them, and make that mechanical cause visible to the facilitator.
- Phrase uncertain interpretation as information the player may evaluate rather than as the protagonist's mandatory conclusion.
- Let fear, loyalty, disgust, trust, moral judgment, and similar internal responses remain player-owned unless the game has a rule that specifically changes them.
- When a premise requires involvement, establish the external condition that creates it — employment, membership, assignment, captivity, obligation, location, or another shared fact — instead of narrating why each protagonist personally cares.
- Review read-aloud and transition text specifically for hidden action verbs or emotions assigned to player characters.

## Don't
- Tell a player that their protagonist chooses, agrees, refuses, trusts, fears, hates, realizes, feels guilty, or decides something merely because the adventure needs that response.
- Use read-aloud prose to move a protagonist, spend their resources, accept an offer, or take another voluntary action without player input.
- Hide a mechanically imposed state inside narration so the table cannot tell whether the response is a rule effect or authorial assumption.
- Treat a shared adventure premise as permission to author every character's private motive or moral interpretation.

## Checklist
- External circumstances and player-controlled responses are clearly distinguishable.
- Any involuntary protagonist state is tied to an explicit rule or established game procedure.
- Read-aloud text does not silently choose voluntary actions or internal judgments for player characters.
- The premise can establish why the group is present without dictating why each protagonist personally values the situation.
- Information is presented so the player can form an interpretation rather than receiving the protagonist's conclusion as narration.

## Notes
Adventure text necessarily controls the world around the protagonists, but it should not quietly become their author. The ownership boundary is simple: the adventure owns external state and NPC action; the player owns voluntary protagonist response unless a game rule explicitly transfers part of that control. Keeping this boundary visible lets authored adventures use strong premises and clear transitions without disguising lost agency as descriptive prose.
