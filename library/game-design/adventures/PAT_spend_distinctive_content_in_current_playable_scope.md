# Spend Distinctive Content in Current Playable Scope

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a strong encounter, threat, puzzle, reveal, location, or other distinctive idea fits the current playable scope and is being deferred mainly from fear that later material will otherwise lack something equally good
**THEN** use the fitting idea in the current adventure or session and reserve delay for cases where setup, sequencing, learned expectation, accumulated stakes, or another real dependency makes later placement function better
**ELSE** keep the idea deferred when its effect genuinely depends on conditions that current play has not earned or established.

## Do
- Audit the current playable span for weak filler that exists only to protect stronger material for a hypothetical future session, level, chapter, or campaign phase.
- Put memorable ideas where the group is actually likely to reach them rather than assuming a distant payoff will definitely be played.
- Let introductory or early material express the game's real identity with distinctive situations instead of replacing that identity with generic safe content solely because the audience is new.
- Distinguish **necessary setup** from **idea hoarding**: preserve delay when information, attachment, escalation, mastery, or contrast is part of what makes the later moment work.
- Spend one strong idea without assuming its exact surface gimmick must remain unique forever; later play can develop new content, consequences, combinations, and variations.
- Judge the current adventure by the experience it delivers within its own likely play horizon, not only by what it promises after many sessions.

## Don't
- Pad early play with repetitive or generic material just to save every unusual idea for later.
- Dump every appealing idea into one encounter when the result loses coherence, readability, or meaningful decision space.
- Reveal a mystery, climax, relationship payoff, or learned threat cue early when the delay itself is doing necessary design work.
- Treat onboarding as a reason to hide the system's defining experience behind bland substitute content.
- Assume future sessions, campaign longevity, or player availability are guaranteed enough to justify withholding the current adventure's strongest fitting material.

## Checklist
- At least one distinctive element is available inside the current playable horizon rather than existing only as a distant promise.
- Any deliberately deferred high-impact element can name the setup, dependency, contrast, or accumulated state that makes later placement stronger.
- Removing filler used only to protect future ideas would not break a required progression or information dependency.
- Introductory or early play still demonstrates something recognizably characteristic of the intended game experience.
- The current unit can be remembered for what players actually did or encountered, not only for what the campaign was preparing to become.

## Notes
A future payoff has value only if play reaches it. Saving every unusual encounter, twist, puzzle, or set piece for a later campaign phase can leave the sessions people actually play carrying the cost while a hypothetical future receives the reward. The useful distinction is not "early good, late bad." Delayed revelation, earned climaxes, accumulated emotional stakes, and mastery-gated complexity can all require patience. This Pattern addresses a different failure: withholding a fitting strong idea merely because the designer fears exhausting the supply of strong ideas.

Starter and introductory material make the distinction especially visible. Simplifying onboarding burden does not require simplifying the game's imaginative promise into generic filler. A newcomer can encounter the real character of the game immediately while the rules, choices, or operating instructions around that experience remain carefully scoped.
