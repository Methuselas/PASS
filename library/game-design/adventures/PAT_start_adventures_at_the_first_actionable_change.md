# Start Adventures at the First Actionable Change

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure opening must move the table from setup into active play
**THEN** begin at or immediately before a concrete change, problem, opportunity, arrival, discovery, or other event that gives players enough context to make a consequential first response
**ELSE** when participation and current action are already established, begin at the first unresolved decision instead of manufacturing a new disturbance.

## Do
- Bring the adventure's live problem or opportunity close enough to the protagonists that they can perceive and act on it without waiting through a long explanatory preamble.
- Make the first decision surface legible: what changed, what is immediately at stake or available, and what the characters can plausibly do next.
- Use motion, danger, arrival, confrontation, discovery, social pressure, celebration, interruption, or another form of change; a strong opening does not require combat.
- Connect the opening to the adventure's participation premise or hook when one is needed, but leave the protagonists' emotional interpretation to their players.
- Give players a reason to engage that they can evaluate in play, such as reward, protection, discovery, duty, relationship, access, status, curiosity, or prevention of a visible loss.
- Trim travelogue and background that can be learned after the first meaningful choice unless that information is required to make the opening decision fairly.

## Don't
- Spend the opening reciting history, patron biography, or setting context while no player decision is possible.
- Treat urgency as permission to prescribe what the protagonists feel, believe, or choose.
- Force one exact response to the opening event when several responses can still support the adventure.
- Begin so late that the characters are already committed to a consequential course they were supposed to choose themselves.
- Add an arbitrary attack, disaster, or theft solely because "something must happen" when an existing unresolved decision already provides momentum.

## Checklist
- Players can identify an actionable change or unresolved situation near the start of play.
- Enough context is available to make a fair first decision without requiring the whole backstory.
- The opening exposes at least one player-evaluable reason to engage when participation is not already guaranteed by the premise.
- More than one plausible response can preserve meaningful play unless the premise honestly constrains the response.
- No protagonist emotion or conclusion is silently authored by the opening text.
- Material before the first consequential decision earns its place by enabling that decision.

## Notes
A useful opening converts premise into momentum. "Something happens" is not valuable because action is inherently superior to quiet play; it is valuable when the change makes the adventure immediately operable. The opening can be an attack, a patron's arrival, a discovered absence, a public event, a blocked route, a strange opportunity, or any other state change that creates a decision. The participation premise still owns why the party is in scope; this Pattern owns when the adventure becomes live at the table.
