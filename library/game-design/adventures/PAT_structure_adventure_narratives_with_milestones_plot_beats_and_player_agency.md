# Structure Adventure Progression with Milestones and Triggered Beats

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure needs authored narrative direction
**THEN** define the required milestones and triggered plot beats, organize how those beats become available, and allow player decisions to change the route and state in which the structural points are reached
**ELSE** do not add mandatory story structure merely because a conventional plot outline expects it.

## Do
- Separate **milestones** from **plot beats**. A milestone is a structural point the adventure needs to reach to progress or stop appropriately; a plot beat is a meaningful story event that occurs within, around, or between those structural points.
- Give important plot beats recognizable triggers such as location, time, player action, discovered information, changed state, or NPC interaction.
- Use maps and locations to organize plot beats spatially when movement through the environment is part of the adventure's structure.
- Allow beats to work at different horizons: some resolve locally, some advance or complicate the current adventure, and some plant information or relationships whose payoff belongs to later play.
- Preserve meaningful choice inside a constrained structure by allowing player decisions to alter route, tactics, relationships, information, resources, risks, conditions, and the state in which later milestones are reached.
- Playtest the architecture for both progress and deviation: observe where groups stall, bypass expected beats, reach milestones in unexpected states, or expose a supposedly meaningful choice whose consequences the adventure cannot support.

## Don't
- Present a choice as consequential and then secretly force every branch back to the same result by erasing what the players did.
- Require the protagonists to make an exact decision when the actual structural requirement is only that play reach a later milestone.
- Confuse a plot beat with a scripted scene that must occur in one exact way regardless of player action.

## Checklist
- Required milestones are distinguishable from optional or conditional plot beats.
- Each important beat has an identifiable trigger or placement condition.
- A map, timeline, clue chain, or other organizing structure makes the availability and ordering of beats executable at the table when needed.
- Players retain meaningful decisions that can change the state in which later beats and milestones are reached.
- Deferred beats are recorded well enough that later adventures can recognize and pay them off.

## Notes
Adventure narrative can establish structural points without scripting the exact story play will produce. Milestones define where progression must eventually arrive; triggered beats define meaningful events that become available through location, time, action, information, state, or interaction. Player decisions can still change the route, cost, relationships, information, and state in which those points are reached.

Adventure plotting often manages tension between authored milestones and location-driven structure. *Star Frontiers: Crash on Volturnus* provides a concrete model of planned encounters that can provide plot and direction while triggering from place, time, or action. *Keep on the Borderlands* likewise uses keyed locations as spatially organized plot beats. The reusable lesson here is to make structural points and their activation conditions explicit enough to run. Entry premises and the force used to preserve supported scope are separate decisions with their own owners.
