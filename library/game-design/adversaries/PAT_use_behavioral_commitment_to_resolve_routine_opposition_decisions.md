# Use Behavioral Commitment to Resolve Routine Opposition Decisions

## Pattern Rule
**IF** ordinary opposition repeatedly faces decisions such as whether to panic, retreat, bargain, surrender, or fight on
**THEN** represent the commitment behind those choices with one compact behavioral abstraction such as morale, discipline, professionalism, doctrine, or mission commitment
**ELSE** use the specific motives and current state of consequential characters when their individual judgment is itself important play.

## Do
- Choose the behavioral abstraction that best matches why this kind of opposition stays engaged or disengages.
- Use it to answer recurring commitment questions consistently instead of improvising a new standard every time the same kind of actor is pressured.
- Let changed circumstances matter when they alter the reason the actor was willing to continue, such as a broken objective, loss of command, overwhelming danger, or a viable bargain.
- Keep the abstraction compact enough that it reduces referee decision load rather than becoming a second personality model.
- Replace or supplement the abstraction with individual motives when an ordinary actor becomes a recurring or consequential character.

## Don't
- Assume every opponent fights to destruction merely because combat has begun.
- Give routine opposition a full psychological model when one commitment variable answers the decisions play repeatedly asks of it.
- Ignore established motives or mission conditions when the abstraction says an actor should continue but the reason for continuing has disappeared.
- Force major adversaries through a generic morale rule when their specific choices are central to the scene.

## Checklist
- The abstraction answers at least one recurring question about continuing, retreating, bargaining, surrendering, or breaking.
- Its meaning is tied to a recognizable cause such as discipline, doctrine, professionalism, or commitment rather than an unexplained number.
- A changed situation can alter the outcome when it removes or strengthens the actor's reason to persist.
- Routine opposition can be resolved without inventing a new behavioral standard scene by scene.
- Consequential characters remain free to use richer individual motives when the scene needs them.

## Notes
A compact behavioral abstraction is useful when the same practical question recurs across many ordinary actors. It preserves coherent opposition behavior while avoiding a full character-equivalent record for every participant. The abstraction is a tool for repeated commitment decisions, not a replacement for individual characterization when a particular NPC becomes important.
