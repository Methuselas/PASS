# Audit Character Economy Exchange Rates Across Creation and Advancement

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the same persistent capabilities can be assembled through different currencies, prices, or purchase timing at character creation and during advancement
**THEN** compare those exchange rates across the character lifecycle and make any timing advantage an intentional strategic or fictional property rather than accidental build-order arbitrage
**ELSE** keep equivalent persistent capability at equivalent economic value when timing itself is not supposed to matter.

## Do
- Compare the price of the same persistent capability at creation and during advancement.
- Name any deliberate reason timing changes value: scarcity, access, opportunity, uncertainty, training time, institutional permission, or another real gameplay difference.
- When creation uses one fungible currency but advancement later splits into experience, money, bodily capacity, access, contacts, downtime, or other currencies, trace which resources are hardest to replace after play begins.
- Test whether optimal construction becomes "buy depth now, breadth later," "buy expensive future tiers now," or another build-order rule driven mainly by exchange-rate asymmetry.
- Compare linear creation pricing with progressive advancement pricing when both can buy the same rating or effect.
- Treat post-creation repair or customization pools as potential arbitrage channels when they can fill cheap low ratings after the shared creation budget has been spent on expensive tiers.
- Re-run the lifecycle audit when new advancement currencies, respec systems, training rules, or purchase permissions are added.

## Don't
- Charge a different price for the same persistent capability solely because it was purchased later when timing has no intended fictional or strategic meaning.
- Repair an underpriced creation option by imposing an arbitrary advancement surcharge while leaving the original leverage unchanged.
- Assume several named currencies are economically independent when players can predictably convert creation choices into later savings.
- Let a concept-repair pool become an optimization pool accidentally because its exchange rate is more favorable than ordinary advancement.

## Checklist
- Representative capabilities have creation and advancement prices compared directly.
- Every intentional timing premium or discount names the gameplay value that justifies it.
- All currencies that replace or supplement the creation budget are included in the lifecycle comparison.
- At least one optimized build sequence is tested for temporal arbitrage.
- Linear and progressive price curves are compared when both apply to the same persistent rating.
- Repair, respec, or customization pools cannot silently produce a better exchange rate than the main economy unless that advantage is intentional.

## Notes
A balanced snapshot can still create a distorted lifecycle. If creation and advancement buy the same persistent capability at different effective exchange rates, build order becomes part of optimization even when the fiction does not support that distinction. The common symptom is a temporal arbitrage rule such as **buy depth now, breadth later** or **spend the one currency that becomes scarce after creation before play begins**. Different prices can be valid, but only when timing, access, scarcity, or opportunity is itself part of the intended play rather than a patch over the base price.
