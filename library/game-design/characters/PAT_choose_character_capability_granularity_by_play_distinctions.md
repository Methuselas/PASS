# Choose Character Capability Granularity by Play Distinctions

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a capability could be represented as a broad package, several narrow traits, numeric levels, categorical proficiency, or an intermediate bundle
**THEN** choose the smallest representation that preserves the distinctions play repeatedly cares about without creating unnecessary acquisition, lookup, or character-management cost
**ELSE** do not add granularity merely because the fiction admits finer distinctions.

## Do
- Compare broad packages with narrow skills by the decisions and character concepts each representation enables in actual play.
- Use broad capabilities when separating their subskills would rarely change who acts, what they can attempt, or how they solve problems.
- Use narrower capabilities when players reasonably need those competencies to differ and the distinction recurs enough to justify separate acquisition and retrieval.
- When one narrow capability is too specific and its parent package is too broad, consider a priced thematic bundle that improves a coherent middle-sized family without collapsing unrelated concepts into the same purchase.
- Use numeric capability levels when adjacent values create distinctions the game repeatedly cares about.
- Use coarse proficiency states when the meaningful questions are categorical and extra numeric resolution would mostly create advancement or dependency overhead.
- Compare the economic price of any middle-sized bundle against both representative individual capabilities and the broader parent trait so the intended level does not become an obvious trap or dominant purchase.

## Don't
- Split a broad capability into many narrow skills without checking whether the increased concept precision is worth the build and retrieval burden.
- Use numeric progression for a capability whose actual play questions are mostly broad proficiency categories.
- Force players toward a broad parent trait merely because buying the intended family of narrow capabilities separately is economically inefficient.
- Preserve a highly granular distinction that almost never changes a decision, permission, consequence, or role in play.

## Checklist
- The chosen granularity matches how often the represented competencies need to differ in play.
- Broad packages do not erase character distinctions the game repeatedly needs.
- Narrow capabilities justify their separate acquisition and retrieval cost.
- Numeric levels remain only where adjacent values matter to recurring play.
- Categorical proficiency is used where it preserves the meaningful distinctions more economically.
- Any intermediate bundle is priced against both its components and its broader parent.

## Notes
Granularity is a cost-and-expression tradeoff, not a ladder from simple to sophisticated. A compact package can make creation and retrieval faster while a narrow skill list can support more exact character concepts. The right resolution is the one that matches the questions play actually asks. When the useful concept sits between a broad attribute and several tiny skills, a thematic bundle can preserve the middle distinction without making the broad parent an automatic economic choice.
