# Express Advancement as Greater Operating Fluency When That Is the Fantasy

## Pattern Rule
**IF** increased expertise is supposed to feel like becoming faster, cheaper, subtler, broader, more controlled, or less constrained rather than only more likely to succeed
**THEN** let advancement improve the operating conditions of the capability in the dimensions that express that mastery
**ELSE** use reliability or magnitude increases when those are the distinctions the game actually wants advancement to represent.

## Do
- Identify what expert performance looks like in the fiction before choosing the advancement axis.
- Let mastery reduce time, ritual, energy, setup, exposure, restrictions, or required support when those are the barriers novices are meant to outgrow.
- When a specialty is meant to be difficult to acquire but fluent once learned, put that difficulty into acquisition, training, prerequisites, or advancement rather than recharging the same rarity cost every time the expert acts.
- Let mastery expand available applications, control, precision, scope, or safe operating range when that is what expertise means in the activity.
- Preserve success-probability advancement when becoming more reliable is itself the intended fantasy.
- Test advanced and novice states in the same representative situation so the difference in operating fluency is visible at the table.
- Keep the improved operating dimension connected to meaningful decisions rather than adding invisible efficiency that never changes play.

## Don't
- Make every step of expertise progression only a larger success number when the intended fantasy is specifically about becoming faster, cheaper, subtler, broader, more controlled, or less constrained.
- Remove all meaningful costs from mastery when those costs are still supposed to structure expert decisions.
- Add many advancement axes merely to make progression look deep when the resulting differences are not experienced in play.
- Make expert runtime procedure deliberately cumbersome merely to signal that the expertise was difficult to obtain when the intended fantasy is fluent mastery.
- Assume lower operating cost is always better when the retained friction is itself part of the capability's identity or risk.

## Checklist
- The fiction of novice and expert performance differs in a way the mechanics can express.
- At least one advancement axis matches that intended form of mastery.
- Advanced use changes a real decision, constraint, opportunity, or application rather than only an invisible number.
- Reliability remains the primary advancement axis only when increased reliability is the experience the design wants.
- A specialty intended to be rare but fluent pays its rarity through acquisition or advancement rather than unnecessary repeated runtime procedure.
- Mastery does not erase pressure the game still intends experts to manage.

## Notes
Advancement need not mean only a higher chance of success. In many activities expertise is experienced as fluency: the same result takes less time, less preparation, less energy, fewer restrictions, or can be achieved in a wider range of circumstances. When that is the fantasy, encode the changed operating conditions directly. This makes mastery visible in how the capability is used rather than only in the final probability.
