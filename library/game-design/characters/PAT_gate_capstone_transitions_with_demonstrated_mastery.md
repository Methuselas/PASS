# Gate Capstone Transitions with Demonstrated Mastery

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a progression boundary is supposed to certify a qualitative transformation in status, role, mastery, or mode of play
**THEN** require a capstone that demonstrates the promised transformation through consequential play in addition to satisfying ordinary numeric prerequisites
**ELSE** use normal advancement when the next step represents more of the same capability rather than a new qualitative state.

## Do
- State what the new status is supposed to prove before designing the gate.
- Choose tasks that exercise the relevant mastery: leadership, creation, breadth, succession, institutional responsibility, world change, difficult judgment, or another capability tied to the transition.
- Let prior advancement establish readiness while the capstone establishes demonstrated fitness for the new state.
- Make the proof produce campaign consequences that remain meaningful whether the transition succeeds immediately or requires further work.
- Support more than one capstone route when the new state can legitimately be earned through different expressions of mastery.

## Don't
- Replace an ordinary level-up with a long quest merely to make high-level progression slower.
- Demand accomplishments unrelated to the identity or permissions the new state will actually grant.
- Make the gate depend entirely on arbitrary institutional scheduling or scarce slots when the intended proof is personal mastery.
- Require the player to repeat already demonstrated competence solely because a numeric threshold was crossed later.

## Checklist
- The qualitative change granted after the gate can be named.
- Each capstone requirement demonstrates a capability, responsibility, or legacy relevant to that change.
- Numeric readiness and demonstrated mastery perform different jobs.
- Failure or delay creates playable consequences rather than only lost time.
- The gate is not being used to certify a transition that is actually just another incremental advancement step.

## Notes
A capstone is valuable when the game wants the next state to mean something that ordinary advancement currency cannot certify. It turns the boundary itself into play: a would-be ruler demonstrates durable rule, a master creator produces something unprecedented, or a candidate for a new mode proves breadth or judgment. This is different from ordinary fictional permission or a progression cost; it is an explicit demonstration that the character can carry the identity the next state represents.
