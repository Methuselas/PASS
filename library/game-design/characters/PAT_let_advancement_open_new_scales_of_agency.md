# Let Advancement Open New Scales of Agency

## Pattern Rule
**IF** long-term advancement is meant to change more than a character's reliability at the same recurring tasks
**THEN** let later progression unlock new categories of consequential action, authority, infrastructure, or persistent world influence and give those new verbs procedures that can sustain play
**ELSE** keep advancement focused on deeper fluency within the existing play scale rather than promising a campaign transformation the rules do not support.

## Do
- Identify the point where improved adventuring competence should become a change in what the character can own, command, build, govern, research, sponsor, produce, or delegate.
- Give newly opened scales their own decisions, costs, opposition, maintenance, and consequences instead of treating them as epilogue text.
- Let earlier character-scale actions remain relevant after the scale widens; scouting, negotiation, sabotage, exploration, and personal intervention can still alter larger persistent systems.
- Connect new authority to campaign state that survives the scene, such as territory, followers, institutions, production capacity, projects, or political relationships.
- Make the transition legible before it arrives so players can decide whether they want to pursue that scale of play.

## Don't
- Treat a title, stronghold, organization, fleet, domain, or similar reward as meaningful advancement if it grants no new decisions.
- Add only larger numerical bonuses when the promised fantasy is that the character has become a ruler, commander, patron, creator, or other actor at a wider scale.
- Force every campaign into domain or institution play merely because high-level characters exist.
- Let newly acquired authority erase the need for player decisions by resolving all subordinate activity automatically.

## Checklist
- At least one later advancement step unlocks a verb that was not meaningfully available at the earlier scale.
- Each new verb has a procedure, cost, constraint, or consequence that can recur in play.
- The larger scale produces persistent state that can change future situations.
- Character-scale actions can still matter after the new scale opens.
- Players can remain at the earlier scale when the campaign does not want the broader mode.

## Notes
Vertical progression and campaign progression are different design axes. A character can become more accurate, durable, or efficient without changing the kind of game being played. A larger transition occurs when advancement grants authority over people, territory, institutions, production, research, armies, worlds, or other persistent systems. That transition is valuable only when the new scale contains decisions worth playing; otherwise it is flavor attached to the same operating loop.
