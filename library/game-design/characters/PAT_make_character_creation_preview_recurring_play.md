# Make Character Creation Preview Recurring Play

## Pattern Rule
**IF** character creation is a player's first sustained encounter with the rules
**THEN** use creation to introduce the vocabulary, choices, resources, and capability relationships that will recur during play
**ELSE** keep creation lightweight when the game does not need it to teach the later operating model.

## Do
- Identify which terms, ratings, resources, tradeoffs, and capability relationships players will repeatedly use after play begins.
- Let creation expose a representative subset of those recurring concepts through actual choices rather than a disconnected tutorial explanation.
- Make creation decisions forecast later behavior: choosing a capability, resource, dependency, or role should help the player understand what that choice will let them do at the table.
- Keep one-time setup concepts clearly separated from recurring play concepts so players know which knowledge must remain operational.
- Observe creation with intended-audience players and check whether the concepts they learn during setup transfer into their first sessions without designer explanation.
- Reduce or defer creation-only procedure that is expensive to learn but contributes little to later decisions unless that one-time construction experience is itself part of the intended fantasy.

## Don't
- Teach an elaborate creation procedure whose terminology and decision structure disappear once play starts merely because character generation can support it.
- Make creation look simpler than play by hiding every recurring resource, dependency, or tradeoff until after the player has committed to a character concept.
- Treat completion of a character sheet as evidence that the player understands how the recorded choices operate in play.
- Add recurring-play concepts to creation solely to expose them early when they do not affect a meaningful creation decision.

## Checklist
- A new player encounters at least some of the same mechanical vocabulary during creation that later appears in ordinary play.
- Major creation choices point toward recognizable later actions, resources, dependencies, or tradeoffs.
- One-time setup concepts are distinguishable from knowledge the player must retain during play.
- Intended-audience players can carry at least part of the creation model into early play without hidden designer explanation.
- Any substantial creation-only complexity has a deliberate value beyond merely making setup more detailed.

## Notes
Character creation is often the first long rules procedure a player performs, so it can either establish the game's operating language or teach a temporary mini-game that must immediately be discarded. The goal is not to force every runtime rule into setup. It is to make the first substantial interaction truthfully preview the choices and concepts that matter later. A deep construction experience can still be worthwhile when building the character is itself part of the intended fantasy; in that case its one-time complexity should be defended on that basis rather than mistaken for onboarding value.
