# Model Collective Identity as Shared Mechanical State

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a party, crew, squad, household, or other player group is supposed to have persistent qualities, resources, tensions, or capabilities that are not reducible to one member's sheet
**THEN** give the collective its own mechanically consequential state that members can contribute to, draw from, and change through play
**ELSE** leave teamwork as interactions among individual characters when the group itself has no decisions or consequences worth tracking.

## Do
- Name what belongs to the collective rather than to a member: shared fortune, cohesion, tension, reputation, doctrine, pooled assets, group abilities, or another state that survives individual actions.
- Give the shared state explicit triggers, limits, refresh conditions, and consequences so it is not merely a facilitator impression of how well the group is behaving.
- Let members contribute distinct capabilities to a common surface when teamwork should make one member's expertise available to the group without copying that capability onto every sheet.
- Let several members spend, activate, protect, or replenish a shared resource when the design wants coordination over ownership to matter.
- Keep personal impairment and personal resources separate when combining them would erase whose capability or consequence is actually changing.
- Decide what happens when membership changes: whether the collective state persists, recalculates, loses contributed abilities, or is replaced.
- Test whether different collective configurations produce different decisions rather than only different labels.

## Don't
- Turn ordinary party composition into another meter unless the group-level state changes choices or consequences independently of individual statistics.
- Use a shared tension or cohesion track as an undefined punishment lever for interpersonal behavior; specify what moves it and what its thresholds do.
- Let a collective ability erase the distinction between members when the game still promises differentiated character roles.
- Duplicate every personal resource into a group pool without a reason for the two ownership levels to behave differently.
- Make one member the permanent gatekeeper for a supposedly shared capability unless that asymmetry is intentional.

## Checklist
- At least one recorded value, ability, resource, or threshold belongs to the group rather than to any single member.
- The collective state can change a decision or outcome even when no individual character statistic changes.
- Contributions and withdrawals have defined owners, triggers, and limits.
- Shared abilities remain distinguishable from the individual capabilities that supplied or enabled them.
- Membership change has an explicit effect on persistent collective state when the campaign permits members to join, leave, or die.
- Removing the group-level object would materially change teamwork, resource allocation, or group consequences rather than merely remove a label.

## Notes
A group can be more than the sum of separate character sheets. A shared surface can make teamwork itself persistent: one character contributes a leadership technique, another contributes a knack, everyone can draw on a common reserve, and accumulated friction can create consequences that belong to the party relationship rather than to one person's stress track. This is not the same as compressing many minor actors into shared state; compression reduces servicing cost for interchangeable entities, while this decision creates an emergent player-owned object whose state is meant to matter in its own right.
