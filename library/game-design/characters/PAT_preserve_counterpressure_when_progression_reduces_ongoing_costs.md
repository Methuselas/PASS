# Preserve Counterpressure When Progression Reduces Ongoing Costs

## Pattern Rule
**IF** advancement, superior equipment, automation, outsourcing, or another progression path can reduce or externalize a capability's ongoing cost
**THEN** price that relief by its full downstream leverage or preserve a replacement scarcity, exposure, dependency, or risk so mastery does not silently turn an exceptional tradeoff into a free baseline
**ELSE** let the cost disappear when removing that pressure is itself the intended progression fantasy.

## Do
- Identify which ongoing costs originally constrained the capability: maintenance, bodily burden, recovery, exposure, attention, supply, evidence, dependency, or another recurring brake.
- Trace each progression option that reduces, transfers, automates, or ignores those costs rather than evaluating upgrades only by their visible numerical bonus.
- Price cost relief by what it enables downstream, including higher activation cadence, safer repetition, freed capacity, reduced vulnerability, or access to combinations that were previously impractical.
- Let superior capital improve efficiency in a scarce capacity when that creates a meaningful long-term goal, but audit whether the efficiency gain removes the original tradeoff completely.
- Introduce replacement pressure only when the experience still needs a constraint; do not preserve friction merely because the low-level version had it.
- Test an advanced build at representative use frequency to see whether the former cost still changes decisions or has become negligible.

## Don't
- Assume an upgrade that removes a penalty is minor because it does not increase the capability's headline effect.
- Let ordinary progression buy away every sustaining, recovery, exposure, or backlash cost while continuing to price the capability as though those brakes still exist.
- Add a new arbitrary tax solely to keep advanced characters from feeling more capable when relief from the old burden is the intended reward.
- Evaluate cost-removal upgrades in isolation when several of them can combine to erase the same constraint.

## Checklist
- The ongoing costs that originally regulate the capability are named.
- Progression options that reduce or externalize those costs are traced through their downstream effects.
- At least one advanced representative build has been tested at realistic activation cadence.
- Any removed pressure is either deliberately part of the progression fantasy or replaced/priced according to the leverage its removal creates.
- Multiple cost-relief options have been checked for whether their combination turns a once-exceptional state into an unpriced baseline.

## Notes
Progression can create power by making the same effect cheaper, safer, or easier to sustain rather than by increasing its raw number. That can be excellent advancement. It becomes a design problem when the original capability was balanced or themed around an ongoing burden that later options quietly erase while every downstream assumption continues to treat the burden as present. Audit relief as leverage. If mastery is supposed to transcend the old limitation, say so; if the limitation is still needed to create the intended pressure, preserve an equivalent constraint or price its removal accordingly.
