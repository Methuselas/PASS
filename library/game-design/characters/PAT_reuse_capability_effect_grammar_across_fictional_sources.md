# Reuse Capability Effect Grammar Across Fictional Sources

## Pattern Rule
**IF** several fictional origins can produce the same or closely related mechanical capability
**THEN** reuse the common effect grammar and express source-specific identity through access, counters, limitations, costs, tags, or advancement only where those differences actually change play
**ELSE** use distinct mechanics when the fictional source creates a genuinely different effect or decision structure.

## Do
- Identify the mechanical effect independently of whether its source is training, magic, cyberware, species, technology, divine favor, mutation, or another fiction.
- Reuse a shared effect when the same inputs, permissions, consequences, and decisions would otherwise be duplicated under different names.
- Attach source-specific counters, vulnerabilities, access requirements, social consequences, costs, or advancement rules when those differences create real play.
- Preserve fictional identity through tags, prerequisites, availability, presentation, or interaction rules instead of duplicating the whole mechanical procedure by default.
- Split the mechanics when the source changes the decision structure, timing, risk, targets, state, or other behavior enough that the common effect is no longer actually common.

## Don't
- Duplicate mechanically identical capabilities into separate subsystems solely because their fictional origins differ.
- Erase source-specific consequences that matter in play merely to maximize mechanical unification.
- Use a common name or shared die roll as proof that two abilities have the same effect grammar when their permissions or consequences materially differ.
- Create parallel advancement trees for identical effects when only the source of access changes.

## Checklist
- The shared mechanical effect can be stated without naming its fictional source.
- Common resolution logic is reused where the effects actually behave the same.
- Source-specific differences are attached only where they change access, counters, costs, identity, consequences, or advancement.
- Fictional distinctions important to play remain visible after reuse.
- A separate subsystem exists only when the source creates a materially different effect or decision structure.

## Notes
Fictional source and mechanical effect are different dimensions. A magical shield, technological barrier, psychic defense, and trained parry may need distinct identities without each requiring an unrelated resolution engine. Reusing the effect grammar reduces duplication while source-specific tags and constraints preserve the world-facing differences that matter. The split should happen only when the source changes what players actually decide or what the effect does.
