# Separate Advancement Price, Permission, and Fictional Cause

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a character can improve, acquire, or unlock a persistent capability
**THEN** decide separately what the new state costs, whether it is currently available to purchase or enter, and what fictional event or condition explains the change
**ELSE** do not let one advancement currency silently answer all three questions when the fiction or balance requires them to differ.

## Do
- Let **price** measure the persistent mechanical value or opportunity cost of the new state.
- Let **permission** state whether the change is currently available given prerequisites, institutions, technology, teachers, rank, access, or other gates.
- Let **fictional cause** explain how the character actually acquires the new state in the shared fiction.
- Require fictional permission or cause when spending points alone would otherwise create a social, institutional, technological, or learned state the setting says must first become possible.
- Track training, exposure, teachers, downtime, or other causal evidence only at the resolution the campaign values.
- Use coarse causal records when they preserve believable advancement without forcing the facilitator to audit every hour or action.

## Don't
- Treat point expenditure alone as sufficient to create a state whose fiction requires access, training, installation, authorization, transformation, or another enabling event.
- Make a fictional prerequisite also pay the full mechanical price unless both costs are intentionally part of the design.
- Require exhaustive training or exposure logs when a coarser causal record would preserve the same believable change.
- Confuse temporary permission to learn with the permanent mechanical value of what is learned.
- Use advancement bookkeeping detail that exceeds the campaign's actual interest in training and causal history.

## Checklist
- Price, permission, and fictional cause can each be stated independently for a representative advancement.
- The price reflects persistent mechanical value rather than substituting for every fictional gate.
- Permission matches the setting's actual access conditions when those matter.
- The fictional cause is sufficient to explain the change without requiring more tracking detail than the campaign values.
- Point expenditure cannot bypass an intentionally required social, technological, institutional, or training state.
- Causal advancement records are as coarse or detailed as later play actually needs.

## Notes
Persistent advancement contains three questions that often get collapsed into one purchase rule: **what is it worth, can I get it now, and how did my character actually get it?** Separating them prevents a price from becoming an accidental permission system and prevents fictional access from being mistaken for balance. It also lets campaigns care about training and institutions at the level of detail they actually use instead of turning believable advancement into exhaustive accounting.
