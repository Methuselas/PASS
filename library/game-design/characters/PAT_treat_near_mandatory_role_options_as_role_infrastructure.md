# Treat Near-Mandatory Role Options as Role Infrastructure

## Pattern Rule
**IF** nearly every effective build for a promised role must buy the same option because it multiplies actions, access, throughput, or another core output
**THEN** treat that option as role infrastructure to grant, bundle, or redesign rather than presenting it as ordinary customization whose omission is supposedly equally viable
**ELSE** keep the option elective when credible role builds can succeed without purchasing it.

## Do
- Build representative versions of the same promised role with and without the suspected tax.
- Compare access, action count, throughput, reliability, and contribution rather than only peak output.
- Identify whether the option is merely strong or whether omitting it changes the character from an effective member of the role into a knowingly deficient build.
- When the purchase is effectively mandatory, consider moving it into the role baseline, bundling it transparently, or redesigning the surrounding dependency that makes it compulsory.
- Preserve genuine customization by charging for choices that differentiate viable builds rather than for permission to meet the role's expected floor.
- Re-test the role after new multipliers, access privileges, or progression options are added.

## Don't
- Call a purchase optional merely because the rules technically permit a character to omit it.
- Defend a role tax only by pointing to its high price when every effective build must still pay that price.
- Hide baseline role competence inside a trap choice that knowledgeable players always buy and inexperienced players can accidentally skip.
- Move every popular option into the baseline; frequency alone does not prove the option is mandatory for effective role performance.

## Checklist
- At least one representative role build with and without the suspected option has been compared.
- The comparison includes access, actions, throughput, and consequential contribution rather than purchase price alone.
- The design can name a credible effective build that omits the option, or it treats the option as infrastructure rather than ordinary customization.
- New players are not punished for failing to discover an unstated mandatory purchase.
- Moving the option into the baseline would not erase a meaningful build distinction that the current economy genuinely supports.

## Notes
A very expensive option can still be a tax rather than a choice. If the promised role only functions at its expected level after buying the same multiplier or access privilege, the economy is charging players for baseline competence and pretending that payment is customization. The diagnostic is counterfactual: **does an informed player have a credible role build that omits this purchase?** If not, make the infrastructure explicit or redesign the dependency so elective purchases can return to differentiating viable builds.
