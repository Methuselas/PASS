# Turn Completed Long-Term Goals into Character Succession

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a character can complete a campaign-scale personal goal that provides a natural endpoint to that character's active arc
**THEN** offer voluntary retirement as a rewarded outcome, preserve the retired character as usable campaign state, and transfer only a bounded legacy benefit to the successor
**ELSE** let the character choose a new long-term goal and continue when retirement would cut off an arc the player still wants to play.

## Do
- Make retirement an option at an earned narrative boundary rather than an arbitrary level, age, or facilitator decision.
- Preserve the achieved goal in the world so retirement changes campaign state instead of deleting the character from continuity.
- Convert the retired character into an NPC, contact, institution, patron, location owner, or another background presence appropriate to what was achieved.
- Give the successor a bounded inheritance of advancement, resources, relationships, or another legacy benefit so changing protagonists does not require a complete experiential reset.
- Keep the inherited value below full transfer when the new character should still have room to establish a distinct identity and progression path.
- Let group-scale goals support coordinated succession when several characters have reached natural endpoints together.

## Don't
- Force a character to retire merely because a stated goal was completed.
- Transfer the entire old character unchanged into a new sheet and call that succession.
- Erase the retired character's achieved position from the campaign when it can provide continuity, support, obligations, or future adventure material.
- Make retirement strictly worse than continuing when the design expects players to consider it a legitimate culmination.
- Require every new protagonist to be a relative, apprentice, or clone of the prior character unless that lineage is part of the intended fiction.

## Checklist
- A long-term goal can reach a state that legitimately closes or transforms the character's active arc.
- The player can choose between continuing with a new goal and retiring when both are supported outcomes.
- Retirement leaves at least one persistent change in campaign state connected to the completed goal.
- The successor receives a bounded benefit that acknowledges prior investment without reproducing the predecessor wholesale.
- The retired character can re-enter the fiction as campaign state without automatically reclaiming protagonist status.

## Notes
Long campaigns can outlive the personal problem that originally drove a character. Treating goal completion as a possible succession point lets a player finish an arc without abandoning the campaign or throwing away all prior investment. The retired protagonist becomes part of the setting the group has built, while a bounded inheritance lowers the cost of taking up a new perspective. Continuing with a fresh goal remains equally important: completion creates an exit opportunity, not an expiration date.
