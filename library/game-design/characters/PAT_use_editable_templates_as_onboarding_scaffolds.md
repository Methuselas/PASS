# Use Editable Templates as Onboarding Scaffolds

## Pattern Rule
**IF** open character construction can overwhelm new players or allow them to omit capabilities their intended role requires
**THEN** provide editable templates, archetypes, or recommended packages that produce competent starting characters while preserving later customization and clear composition rules
**ELSE** do not turn a convenience scaffold into a mandatory permission boundary.

## Do
- Build templates from competent examples of recurring roles or concepts rather than from cosmetic labels alone.
- Make the scaffold editable so a player can customize the concept without first reconstructing the entire option catalog.
- Keep role scaffolds distinct from mandatory ontological packages such as species, body-state, or other traits the setting treats as inherent rather than optional role guidance.
- When templates, lenses, packages, or reusable trait groups can compose, define duplicate, precedence, conflict, and merge behavior.
- State which elements are recommendations, which are required by the template's promise, and which can be exchanged safely.
- Test that a novice can produce a competent starting character from the scaffold without mastering the whole construction system first.

## Don't
- Treat an editable role template as a hard permission boundary merely because it is a convenient onboarding device.
- Hide essential role competence in optional-looking template pieces that a novice can remove without understanding the consequence.
- Let stacked templates silently double-charge, duplicate, overwrite, or contradict the same trait because merge rules were never defined.
- Use a template label as proof that the resulting character can actually perform the role the label promises.

## Checklist
- At least one scaffold produces a competent starting character for its advertised role.
- Players can identify which elements are editable without accidentally removing hidden prerequisites.
- Templates remain guidance unless the game deliberately intends them to create hard permissions.
- Composable packages state how duplicates, leveled traits, contradictions, and optional variation are merged.
- The scaffold reduces entry burden without requiring the player to understand the entire option catalog before making a viable character.

## Notes
Templates can provide an interface over open construction rather than a separate character ontology. They are most useful when they preserve competence and reduce the number of decisions a newcomer must make before play, while remaining editable once the player understands the system. Their composition rules matter as soon as several packages can stack; a convenience layer becomes a source of hidden bugs if duplicate and conflict behavior is left implicit.
