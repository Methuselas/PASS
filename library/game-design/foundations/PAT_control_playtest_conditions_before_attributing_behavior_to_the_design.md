# Control Playtest Conditions Before Attributing Behavior to the Design

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a playtest is meant to produce evidence about how the game behaves
**THEN** establish the kind of play being tested, the participant expectations, and the table conditions needed for that content before attributing confusion, disengagement, delay, or other behavior to the design
**ELSE** label uncontrolled conditions as a possible confound instead of treating the session as clean causal evidence.

## Do
- State what kind of experience, content, subsystem, or scenario the test is exercising so participants are not unknowingly measured against an unstated expectation.
- Use safety, consent, and table-management procedures appropriate to the content, intended audience, and test environment.
- Record material environmental conditions that could affect the observation, such as missing tools, disrupted communication, inappropriate group size, platform failure, unclear facilitation responsibility, or a mismatch between announced and actual content.
- Distinguish friction produced by the written game from friction introduced by the way the test was staged or facilitated.
- Rerun or qualify important findings when a material confound makes the original attribution uncertain.
- Preserve unusual but intentional test conditions when they model a real target environment; control means making the condition explicit, not forcing every test into one ideal table setup.

## Don't
- Treat participant confusion about undisclosed goals or content as clean evidence that the underlying mechanic is confusing.
- Use missing safety or table-management procedures to create avoidable participant distress and then classify the resulting behavior as design evidence.
- Erase a real environmental limitation merely to make the test cleaner when that limitation is part of the intended use case.
- Hide known confounds when summarizing a finding because the result matches the designer's prior belief.

## Checklist
- Participants know what kind of play or content the session is testing at the level needed to engage with it appropriately.
- Table procedures are appropriate to the content and audience being tested.
- Material environmental or facilitation confounds are recorded when they occur.
- Findings distinguish game-produced friction from test-condition friction where the evidence allows that distinction.
- A consequential finding produced under a material confound is rerun, qualified, or left unresolved rather than promoted directly into a design conclusion.

## Notes
A playtest is an observation of the game operating inside a social and technical environment, not a laboratory vacuum. Expectations, facilitation, communication, platform reliability, and participant-support procedures can all change what the table does. The goal is not sterile testing. It is attribution discipline: make relevant conditions explicit so the designer can tell whether a behavior belongs to the game, to the environment the game must genuinely support, or to a staging failure that should not become canonical evidence about the design.
