# Make Reversible Character Death Costly and Conditional

## Pattern Rule
**IF** character death is intended to remain a real consequence but the campaign also permits a dead character to return
**THEN** make return depend on visible conditions and meaningful costs such as recoverable remains, scarce services, preparation, time, resources, obligations, exposure, or another consequential recovery path
**ELSE** let death remain terminal or use replacement and alternate-participation structures appropriate to the game's promised mortality model.

## Do
- Decide before play what makes return possible: body recovery, a spell or device, specialist access, a prepared duplicate, a rare resource, a bargain, a location, a time window, or another explicit condition.
- Make the important prerequisites learnable enough that players can prepare for mortality rather than discovering the recovery model only after a death occurs.
- Price return by the campaign-scale value of preserving the character, not by a token fee that high-level play can ignore automatically.
- Let recovery cost create further decisions through travel, debt, lost resources, political attention, vulnerability, or other consequences when those fit the setting.
- Preserve the distinction between **reversing death after it occurs** and **preventing a terminal result before it occurs**; the latter is owned by catastrophic-failure protection.
- Provide a participation bridge when the recovery procedure would otherwise leave the player inactive longer than the campaign can comfortably absorb.
- Re-test the loop at advanced play. Wealth, access, automation, or repeated resurrection can turn a formerly serious consequence into routine maintenance if the recovery costs stop mattering.

## Don't
- Make revival effectively free and automatic while still expecting death to create strong caution, preparation, or tension.
- Require an obscure recovery prerequisite that players had no reasonable way to know before the loss.
- Assume monetary cost alone remains meaningful after progression has made that amount trivial.
- Erase every consequence of the fatal event merely because the character eventually returns.
- Confuse a recovery system with a guarantee that every death must be reversed; players can still decline, fail to meet, or lose access to the return conditions.

## Checklist
- The conditions that permit return from death are explicit and operable.
- Players can learn the major preparation requirements before a death forces the issue.
- At least one cost or consequence of return remains meaningful at the campaign tier where the option is expected to be used.
- The player has a known participation path if recovery takes substantial table time.
- Repeated access to revival has been checked for whether it turns mortality into a negligible reset loop.
- The design distinguishes post-death recovery from pre-death continuity protection and from ordinary character replacement.

## Notes
Reversible death can preserve long-term character continuity without making mortality meaningless. The useful pressure comes from conditions around return: recovering a body, obtaining rare or expensive help, preparing a duplicate, spending a powerful resource, accepting an obligation, or exposing the character to new attention. These costs need not all appear at once, and death does not need to be common. The important question is whether the possibility of return still leaves players with reasons to care about avoiding death before it happens.
