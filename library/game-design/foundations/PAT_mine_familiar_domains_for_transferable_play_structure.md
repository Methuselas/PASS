# Mine Familiar Domains for Transferable Play Structure

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a game needs a grounded activity loop, institution, relationship, resource flow, or recurring situation and the designer knows an external domain well
**THEN** isolate the domain's operating relationships and functions, translate the useful ones into player-facing actions, resources, constraints, and institutions, then rebuild them to serve the destination game's experience
**ELSE** research or generate from the game's own requirements rather than importing a domain merely because it is familiar.

## Do
- Start from what the familiar domain actually does: who has which roles, what moves between them, what risks or obligations recur, what information matters, and what causes people to return.
- Identify functions that can become play, such as brokerage, resupply, appraisal, transport, recovery, contracting, reputation, security, collection, repair, or information exchange.
- Translate those functions into decisions and consequences the players can act on rather than using the source domain only as decorative lore.
- Change scale, technology, culture, legality, economics, and procedure as needed so the transferred structure fits the destination game's rules and intended experience.
- Keep only the pieces that generate useful play; familiarity is a source of causal detail, not a requirement to simulate the original domain completely.
- Test whether the imported structure creates recurring choices, dependencies, or opportunities that would still be useful if its historical or real-world labels were removed.

## Don't
- Copy surface names, costumes, or history while leaving the underlying activity irrelevant to play.
- Assume a structure is balanced, fun, fair, or comprehensible in a game merely because it functioned in a real institution or another medium.
- Preserve source-domain complexity that does not create a decision, pressure, resource, relationship, or consequence in the destination game.
- Let enthusiasm for the familiar subject override the game's intended player fantasy or recurring activity.

## Checklist
- The borrowed domain can be described in functional relationships rather than only recognizable imagery.
- At least one transferred function becomes a concrete player action, resource decision, access route, obligation, or recurring opportunity.
- The implementation has been rebuilt for the destination game's rules, scale, and intended experience.
- Removing the source-domain labels would still leave a coherent playable structure.
- Unnecessary simulation detail has been cut instead of being preserved as proof of research.

## Notes
Familiar subject matter can supply more than flavor. Knowing how a trade, craft, workplace, historical institution, hobby community, or other practice actually operates exposes roles, dependencies, exchanges, recurring problems, and return loops that can be translated into play. A frontier trading post, for example, is interesting not because it is historical but because brokerage, supply, appraisal, information, security, and repeat relationships can all become player-facing functions.

Transfer is a design act rather than replication. Real institutions optimize for real constraints, not for pacing, fairness, clarity, or the intended fantasy of a game. Keep the causal structure that produces useful decisions and rebuild the rest.
