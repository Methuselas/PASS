# Reuse Mechanical Function Under a New Fictional Skin

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an established creature, item, ability, ancestry, spell effect, or other game element already provides the mechanical behavior the design needs but its fictional identity is too familiar or does not fit the intended presentation
**THEN** preserve the useful mechanical function, replace the fictional skin, and reconcile every rule interaction that the new identity materially changes
**ELSE** change or replace the mechanics when the new fiction should create a different play pattern rather than merely a different appearance

## Do
- State which mechanical behaviors are being preserved before changing the element's name, appearance, origin, or fictional role.
- Use established mechanics to carry familiar operating behavior while the new fiction supplies novelty, mystery, or setting fit.
- Audit category-dependent interactions after the reskin: creature type, material, physiology, source, vulnerabilities, immunities, targeting permissions, or other tags may no longer make sense unchanged.
- Add only the mechanical differences the new fiction actually requires, rather than rebuilding the entire element because one interaction changed.
- Use reskinning selectively among familiar elements so the altered pieces remain distinctive instead of turning every encounter, reward, or ability into the same novelty exercise.
- When the reskin expands into several consequential mechanical changes, treat the result as a modified or new element and evaluate it on that basis rather than continuing to call the change cosmetic.

## Don't
- Invent a new subsystem when an established mechanical package already produces the desired behavior and only the fictional identity needs to change.
- Preserve old categories, immunities, vulnerabilities, or targeting rules when they contradict the new fictional form.
- Assume a different name or description creates a different play pattern when every consequential interaction remains identical.
- Reskin every available element merely to demonstrate originality; repeated novelty can become as predictable as the stock material it replaced.

## Checklist
- The preserved mechanical function can be stated independently of the old fictional identity.
- The new fictional identity is clear enough that players and facilitators can understand what the element is in the world.
- Every rule that depends on category, material, physiology, source, or another changed fictional property has been checked for consistency.
- Any added vulnerability, immunity, permission, or limitation follows from the new identity rather than from a desire to make the reskin seem more different.
- The element either preserves the intended play pattern at lower design cost or has been reclassified as a mechanically changed design when its new fiction requires different decisions.
- Reskinned content is mixed with enough stable material that novelty remains legible rather than becoming uniform noise.

## Notes
Reskinning separates **what an element does in play** from **what it is in the fiction** long enough to reuse a proven mechanical package. The separation is not absolute. A giant scorpion recast as a clockwork excavation machine can preserve multi-attack, armor, grabbing, and a dangerous injector while no longer behaving like an animal for rules that care about creature type; its constructed body may also justify different material interactions. Preserve what still fits, change what the new identity actually changes, and stop calling the result a pure reskin once those changes create a different decision structure.
