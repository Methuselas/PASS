# Test Audience Fit with Representative External Players

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a playtest is being used to judge whether the game serves its intended players
**THEN** include testers who resemble that audience but are not limited to the designer's regular group, vary relevant familiarity within the target, and classify audience mismatch separately from design-performance failure
**ELSE** label tests with intentionally different players as boundary or transfer evidence rather than treating them as direct proof of target-audience fit.

## Do
- Recruit testers who match the intended audience on the traits that actually affect use: rules familiarity, age or experience when relevant, tolerance for reference work, genre expectations, and the kind of play they seek.
- Include people who did not learn the game through the designer's regular table so hidden conventions and compensating habits have a chance to surface.
- Vary relevant experience and play style inside the target population instead of treating one familiar group as a miniature of the whole audience.
- Record whether a negative response concerns execution of the promised experience or dislike of the promised experience itself.
- Use out-of-audience testers deliberately when they can expose assumptions or transfer limits, but keep that evidence labeled so it does not silently redefine the target.
- Revisit the audience definition when repeated target-fit evidence shows that the people who can operate and enjoy the actual design differ from those the project originally claimed to serve.

## Don't
- Treat the designer's home group as sufficient audience representation because they already understand the rules and enjoy the campaign.
- Redesign toward every negative preference report without first asking whether the tester wanted the experience the game was built to provide.
- Dismiss all out-of-audience feedback as useless; it can reveal assumptions even when it does not establish target fit.
- Let broad public reach retroactively redefine the intended audience for the purpose of evaluating the design.

## Checklist
- At least some completed-game testing comes from intended-audience players outside the designer's regular table.
- The sample varies at least one relevant familiarity or play-style dimension within the intended audience.
- Negative evidence can be classified as audience mismatch, design-performance failure, or unresolved rather than being collapsed into one category.
- Out-of-audience evidence is labeled and interpreted as boundary or transfer evidence when that is what it represents.
- The current intended-audience definition remains consistent with the people whose experience the design is actually being judged against.

## Notes
A regular group is valuable evidence but a poor stand-in for an audience because it accumulates shared vocabulary, facilitator habits, and workarounds that newcomers do not possess. Representative testing therefore needs both target fit and some distance from the design team's normal table. The opposite mistake is equally costly: a tester can accurately dislike an experience that the game intentionally promises. That reaction may define the boundary of the audience without showing a design failure. Audience-fit testing is strongest when sampling and interpretation preserve that distinction.
