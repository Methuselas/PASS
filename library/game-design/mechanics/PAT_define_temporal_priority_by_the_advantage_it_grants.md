# Define Temporal Priority by the Advantage It Grants

## Pattern Rule
**IF** several participants can form competing intentions inside the same decision window and order can change the resulting choices or state
**THEN** define what temporal priority actually grants before choosing the initiative or ordering method that assigns it
**ELSE** use simultaneous, side-based, fixed, or otherwise simplified ordering when individual priority does not create a meaningful advantage.

## Do
- Name the benefit of priority explicitly: earlier resolution, later commitment, information advantage, reaction rights, interruption authority, positional access, extra actions, denial of opposition, or another timing benefit.
- Choose random rolls, fixed ratings, declaration order, action or weapon speed, side order, simultaneous resolution, passes, delays, interrupts, or another method based on the priority behavior the game needs.
- Separate acting earlier from acting more often; a speed or initiative value that buys both carries more leverage than a simple ordering statistic.
- Check whether later declaration can be an advantage when it reveals earlier intentions even if the action resolves later.
- Make delay, interrupt, reaction, and simultaneous-resolution rules explicit when they can reverse or bypass ordinary priority.
- Test whether early success removes or degrades later opposition strongly enough to create a first-strike feedback loop.

## Don't
- Choose an initiative method only because it is conventional for the genre or dice system.
- Treat a bonus to acting earlier as equivalent to extra actions when the game grants those benefits separately.
- Ignore information advantage created by declaration order merely because resolution order is unchanged.
- Add detailed individual ordering when the resulting priority has no meaningful effect on decisions or consequences.

## Checklist
- The concrete advantage of acting, declaring, or reacting earlier or later can be named.
- The ordering method assigns that advantage in a way consistent with the intended uncertainty and character capabilities.
- Action quantity, reaction rights, and priority are distinguishable even when one statistic affects more than one of them.
- Delay, interrupt, and simultaneous cases have a defined relationship to ordinary order when the game supports them.
- A first-strike advantage has been checked for whether it compounds by reducing the opponent's later opportunity to act.

## Notes
Initiative is not one universal mechanic. Different systems roll for order, use fixed attributes, compare weapon or action speed, alternate sides, declare before resolving, resolve simultaneously, allow delay and interrupts, or split a round into multiple passes. Those methods matter because priority grants something. The design decision begins by naming that benefit, then choosing an ordering method that creates the intended tactical and informational structure without accidentally bundling acting first, acting more often, and reacting more often into one unexamined advantage.
