# Discharge Accumulated Pressure Through Voluntary Complications

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an accumulating harmful state is meant to shape play before it reaches a threshold or collapse result
**THEN** offer the player concrete, thematically causal complications that remove a bounded amount of pressure when accepted, while leaving the pressure unchanged when the player refuses
**ELSE** let the pressure resolve only through its ordinary threshold and recovery procedures when intermediate complications would not create useful decisions.

## Do
- Make each offered complication express the source of the pressure: corruption should tempt or betray, debt should create collection pressure, guilt should complicate relationships, and so on.
- Keep acceptance voluntary when the complication changes a player-controlled character's chosen action or conduct.
- State the exact amount of pressure discharged so the player can compare the immediate complication against the future risk of keeping the load.
- Let refusal preserve the accumulated pressure rather than adding a second punishment for saying no.
- Choose complications that create executable state now: an enemy escapes, an ally is endangered, a duty is neglected, information is exposed, a resource is lost, or another concrete consequence enters play.
- Keep the discharge bounded so repeated minor complications cannot erase a major accumulated threat for trivial cost.

## Don't
- Present a supposedly voluntary complication after the facilitator has already decided the character performs it.
- Let refusal increase the pressure unless that additional escalation is a separately stated mechanic rather than coercion disguised as choice.
- Trade pressure for flavor-only behavior that changes no current or future decision.
- Offer consequences unrelated to the pressure's fiction merely because they are inconvenient.
- Allow one accepted complication to erase the whole track when the accumulated state is intended to remain consequential.

## Checklist
- The player can accept or refuse the complication before the affected character action is fixed.
- Acceptance produces a concrete consequence and removes a stated, bounded amount of pressure.
- Refusal leaves the ordinary pressure state intact without an extra hidden penalty.
- The complication is recognizably caused by the same fiction or theme that created the pressure.
- Representative offers include at least one case where accepting is rational and one where preserving the pressure is rational.

## Notes
Threshold systems often leave accumulated pressure mechanically quiet until the threshold is crossed. Voluntary discharge gives that latent state a second way to matter: the player can convert future systemic danger into an immediate complication. Because the player retains the right to refuse, the mechanic can let corruption, temptation, obligation, or another pressure push on protagonist behavior without silently transferring authorship of the character to the facilitator. The pressure remains meaningful because refusing the bargain leaves the original risk in place.
