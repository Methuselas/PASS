# Make Resource Retention Pay for Persistence Across Cycles

## Pattern Rule
**IF** accumulated surplus is supposed to be temporary because carrying it freely across campaign cycles would erase the intended scarcity or lifestyle pressure
**THEN** define a clear cycle boundary where unsecured surplus is spent, lost, or otherwise removed, and give players explicit ways to preserve it by paying opportunity, access, risk, or another meaningful persistence cost
**ELSE** let resources remain persistent when long-term accumulation is part of the intended progression fantasy.

## Do
- State the reset boundary before players commit to earning or holding the resource so disappearance is a known economy rule rather than a surprise confiscation.
- Give players at least one preservation route that competes with another valuable use of downtime, access, safety, or capital.
- Let preservation methods differ in tradeoff: a secure option may require status and extra actions, while an accessible option may expose the stockpile to theft or loss.
- Connect the removed surplus to the fiction through living costs, repairs, taxes, debts, theft, gifts, consumption, or another campaign-appropriate sink when that helps maintain setting identity.
- Preserve purchased durable assets or other categories the reset is not meant to target; define the resource boundary precisely.
- Test whether the retention cost restores recurring scarcity without making earning surplus feel pointless.

## Don't
- Delete accumulated resources between sessions without telling players that persistence itself is constrained.
- Offer a preservation method so cheap and reliable that the reset rule never creates a decision.
- Make every preservation route depend on one character build or social class unless unequal access is an intended campaign pressure.
- Reset durable progress, equipment, relationships, and liquid surplus together merely because they share a monetary value.
- Use cycle decay in a game whose core reward fantasy is permanent accumulation and compounding wealth.

## Checklist
- The campaign cycle boundary and the resource categories affected by it are explicit.
- At least one preservation method consumes a scarce opportunity, accepts risk, requires access, or pays another meaningful cost.
- A representative player can rationally choose both to preserve surplus and to let it go in different circumstances.
- The reset restores a recurring scarcity or lifestyle pressure that would otherwise disappear after several successful cycles.
- Durable accomplishments outside the targeted surplus remain persistent unless the design explicitly says otherwise.

## Notes
A campaign economy can collapse after enough successful adventures if liquid surplus persists without friction. A cycle-bound retention rule makes persistence itself a decision: spend the surplus freely now, invest scarce downtime in protecting it, accept risk for easier access, or let ordinary life consume it. This is a stronger intervention than ordinary upkeep and should be used only when recurring scarcity is part of the promised experience. The useful pressure comes from the preservation choices, not from arbitrary confiscation.
