# Build a Playable Setting

## Objective
Build enough setting to support recurring character action, resource and access choices, risks, institutions, locations, and adventure pressure without turning worldbuilding into an encyclopedia or propagating dramatic premises beyond their actual dependencies.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Enter with the game's intended experience and current scope.** The setting must serve a known kind of play. If the project cannot say what characters are expected to do or what the current product needs the world to support, return to foundation design before expanding lore.
2. **Translate genre only when a genre promise constrains the world.** If a recognizable genre or subgenre is part of the game contract, use **Translate Genre into Play Requirements** to identify the power relationships, threats, scarcity, mobility, institutions, and other play requirements the setting must make possible. If genre is not carrying a design promise, skip this branch instead of inventing obligations from labels.
3. **Allocate detail by contact with play.** Use **Spend Worldbuilding Detail Where It Changes Play** to decide which cultures, institutions, resources, customs, travel systems, daily-life details, and other elements deserve development. Gate expansion on a visible route into player understanding, choice, atmosphere, adventure utility, or facilitator use; leave low-impact areas lightly sketched.
4. **Propagate major assumptions through real dependencies.** For each setting fact that materially changes what inhabitants or characters can do, use **Propagate World Assumptions Along Actual Dependencies** to state its operating limits and trace only the travel, commerce, institutions, resources, information, character options, or adventure structures that actually depend on it. If an apparent contradiction has no real dependency, do not patch it merely for thematic uniformity.
5. **Turn important places and institutions into action surfaces.** Use **Build Playable Settings as Action Topologies** to define heavily used locations partly by what characters can do there, what resources and information they expose, what risks and access conditions apply, and how relationships or roles change their value. If two detailed places have the same practical function, either differentiate their affordances or reduce redundant detail.
6. **Preserve intentional open space when building inside an established world.** If the setting deliberately leaves areas for referees or later creators to own, use **Preserve Deliberate Setting Negative Space for Downstream Ownership** before filling them. Add canon only when the playable relationships, pressures, or context it creates outweigh the authorship space it removes. If no downstream ownership value exists, define the area normally.
7. **Expose construction grammar only when extension is expected use.** If referees, players, modders, or later authors are expected to create campaign-central setting content, use **Expose Construction Grammar for Expected Downstream Authorship** to provide stable fields, constraints, baselines, dependency rules, and examples. If users only consume the setting or make bounded local changes, do not create a construction system merely for completeness.
8. **Run the playability gate.** Sample the setting from the position of a character with a real need: treatment, work, travel, information, equipment, shelter, status, negotiation, concealment, or another recurring objective. The setting must expose actionable differences in where to go, whom to approach, what it costs, what can go wrong, or what relationships matter. If the answer remains pure lore, return to the detail-allocation or action-topology owner.
9. **Close at sufficient world support, not encyclopedic completion.** The action is complete when the scoped setting has enough causal assumptions, playable locations/institutions, relevant detail, and extension guidance or open space to support the promised play without requiring hidden world logic. Undeveloped material outside that need remains legitimate future or downstream ownership rather than evidence that the setting is unfinished.

## Notes
The protocol builds outward from play contact rather than from a demand to describe everything. Genre translation, preservation of negative space, and downstream construction grammar are conditional branches. A compact setting can be complete for play when its important assumptions propagate coherently and its locations expose useful verbs; a huge setting can still be inert if its detail never changes decisions.
