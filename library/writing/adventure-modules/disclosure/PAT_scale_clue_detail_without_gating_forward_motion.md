# Scale Clue Detail Without Gating Forward Motion

## Pattern Rule
**IF** later adventure material requires the characters to learn a lead but their degree of discovery can legitimately vary
**THEN** write a clue ladder whose minimum usable result preserves forward motion while stronger results add specificity, warning, leverage, interpretation, or safer preparation
**ELSE** use a single direct reveal when variable information would not create a meaningful difference in play

## Do
- Identify the minimum actionable lead the characters need in order to choose a next step.
- Write progressively richer clue tiers that all point toward the same essential route while changing how confidently or safely the characters can follow it.
- Let stronger discovery name more precise locations, actors, hazards, relationships, timing, or implications rather than merely repeating the same fact with more adjectives.
- Make the best clue materially useful without making it the sole key that permits later content to exist.
- State what the operator gives at each tier so partial success does not require improvising a replacement clue on the spot.
- If even the minimum clue can be completely missed, provide another credible opportunity to recover the essential lead.

## Don't
- Put the only route to required later material behind one binary check or all-or-nothing interpretation.
- Call two clue tiers different when both produce the same practical knowledge.
- Make weak results so vague that players still cannot identify a plausible next action.
- Make strong results purely ornamental when they are supposed to reward better discovery.
- Solve the whole mystery at the first tier when later inquiry is meant to remain meaningful.

## Checklist
- The weakest successful tier gives enough information for a concrete next action.
- Each stronger tier adds a distinct practical advantage such as precision, warning, context, or preparation.
- All tiers preserve the same essential continuity unless the adventure intentionally branches.
- The strongest tier rewards success without being required merely to continue.
- The operator can deliver each tier without inventing missing connective information.
- A total miss, if possible, has an alternate source or later recovery opportunity for indispensable information.

## Notes
Adventure clues can carry both progression and reward. When essential information has several degrees of clarity, partial success need not mean narrative failure: a vague but actionable direction can keep the scenario moving, while better discovery can reveal a name, a destination, a hazard, or an interpretive advantage. This is different from simply partitioning hidden information by reveal state. Reveal-state writing controls when information may cross to the players; a clue ladder controls how much usable detail crosses when the degree of discovery varies.
