# Test an Adventure Draft Through Independent Operator Handoff

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure manuscript is meant to be run by someone other than its author
**THEN** give an independent operator the same draft, maps, and supporting material intended for release, let that person prepare and run from the artifact, and revise places where successful operation depended on knowledge that existed only in the author's head
**ELSE** label author-led testing as evidence about the adventure in play rather than proof that the manuscript itself can carry the handoff

## Do
- Use a version of the text and maps close enough to the intended release form that missing explanations, bad references, and unclear transitions can surface.
- Let another operator prepare from the artifact rather than from an oral briefing that silently supplies context the manuscript does not contain.
- Watch for questions, stalls, contradictory readings, improvised repairs, missing setup, and moments where the operator cannot tell what information or procedure applies next.
- Ask what the operator found confusing, dull, predictable, or unexpectedly difficult to use, then distinguish a manuscript problem from a personal preference before revising.
- Treat repeated author explanation as evidence that the handoff surface is incomplete or unclear unless the manuscript explicitly delegates that judgment.

## Don't
- Run every test yourself and assume that smooth facilitation proves another person can reconstruct the same scenario from the text.
- Coach the operator through missing material and still count the run as proof that the draft is self-sufficient.
- Rewrite every passage merely because another operator would phrase or improvise it differently.
- Convert player enjoyment, encounter balance, or rules-performance feedback automatically into a writing defect when the manuscript communicated the intended design correctly.

## Checklist
- At least one operator who did not author the manuscript has prepared and run from the intended draft package.
- Any author explanation required for preparation or continuation has been recorded rather than disappearing into the session.
- Confusing references, missing transitions, unstated assumptions, and other handoff failures have a specific textual repair or are deliberately delegated by the manuscript.
- The operator can identify the adventure's setup, relevant local information, and next usable procedure without reconstructing private author intent.

## Notes
An author-run playtest can reveal whether an encounter works while still concealing whether the manuscript works. The author already knows where the clue is, how two rooms relate, what an abbreviated note means, and which unstated assumption repairs a gap. A second operator removes that private bridge. The useful evidence is not whether the second operator reproduces the author's personal style; it is whether the artifact gives that operator enough information to prepare, interpret, and continue without borrowing unwritten intent.
