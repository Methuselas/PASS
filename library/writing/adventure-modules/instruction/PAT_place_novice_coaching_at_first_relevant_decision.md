# Place Novice Coaching at the First Relevant Decision

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a novice operator must make a non-obvious judgment while running an adventure
**THEN** place concise coaching at the first live occurrence of that judgment, state the principle the operator should notice, demonstrate it in the immediate situation, and let later repetitions rely increasingly on the learned principle
**ELSE** keep routine instructions in reference material when no contextual example is needed to make them usable

## Do
- Identify the first moment where a novice is likely to ask what to describe, what information to reveal, how an actor should respond, or how a prior action should change the situation.
- Put the lesson adjacent to that moment so the operator does not have to remember an abstract lecture delivered many pages earlier.
- State the reusable principle before or alongside the local answer: for example, describe only what can be perceived, let speech and conduct affect later reactions, or let intelligent actors respond according to what they know.
- Use the current scene as the worked instance, explaining which details demonstrate the principle and which are specific to this one situation.
- Reduce repetition after the first few demonstrations; later keyed material should assume the principle unless a new exception or higher-order judgment appears.
- Cross-reference a concise general rule when the same judgment recurs often, but keep the first contextual demonstration where the novice encounters the problem.

## Don't
- Concentrate every operational lesson in an introductory essay and assume a beginner will retrieve the right paragraph under live pressure.
- Put crucial coaching after the scene in which it was first needed.
- Give only the local ruling without exposing the principle that would let the operator handle a similar unprepared case later.
- Repeat the same full lesson beside every occurrence after the operator has already been taught what to do.
- Turn point-of-use coaching into a script that answers every possible player action in advance.
- Mix several unrelated lessons into one sidebar merely because they occur on the same page.

## Checklist
- Every non-obvious novice judgment has a first-use location where the relevant coaching is easy to find.
- The first example teaches a transferable principle, not just a one-off answer.
- The example clearly separates scenario facts from the operator judgment being modeled.
- Later occurrences require less explanatory support unless the situation introduces a new complication.
- A novice can apply the lesson to at least one plausible unprepared variation without finding another special-case instruction.
- The instructional note does not interrupt player-facing material or leak hidden information into it.

## Notes
A runnable module is read under different conditions from a manual. The operator often discovers that a concept matters only when a scene demands a decision. Point-of-use coaching therefore treats the prepared adventure itself as the retrieval surface: the first gate conversation can teach social consequence, the first hidden feature can teach information restraint, and the first adaptive opponent can teach how prior player behavior changes later responses.

The lesson should fade after it has done its work. Repeating the entire explanation forever keeps the module dependent on instruction instead of building operator judgment. The goal is transfer: the next similar case should be easier even when the author did not prewrite it.
