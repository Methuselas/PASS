# Structure Encounter Entries for Live Retrieval

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a facilitator must consult an encounter entry repeatedly while the scene is unfolding
**THEN** organize the entry with a predictable retrieval grammar that separates immediate player-facing observations from operator-facing state, special mechanics, actor capabilities and behavior, rewards, and downstream consequences, omitting sections that do not apply
**ELSE** use ordinary narrative organization when the material will be read once rather than queried during live operation

## Do
- Lead with the observations characters can immediately perceive or that the facilitator may need to paraphrase before players act.
- Put physical conditions, hidden state, triggers, hazards, and other operator-only facts close to the point where they become relevant.
- Isolate any special rule, trap, puzzle, or unusual resolution procedure so the facilitator can find the mechanism without rereading descriptive prose.
- Keep actor statistics and capabilities near the behavior or tactics that depend on them instead of scattering one encounter across distant sections.
- Put recoverable rewards, clues, exits, and follow-on developments where the facilitator will look after the immediate action resolves.
- Use the same broad section order across similar encounters, but omit empty headings rather than filling a template ceremonially.

## Don't
- Bury a critical trigger, immunity, tactic, or consequence inside a paragraph whose main job is atmosphere.
- Separate the information needed for one decision across distant pages when it can be colocated without duplication.
- Force the facilitator to search a rulebook or another chapter for a routine fact the encounter can state compactly at the point of use.
- Mix player-facing description and hidden operator explanation so thoroughly that paraphrasing risks accidental disclosure.
- Add headings that carry no information merely to make every encounter look identical.

## Checklist
- The facilitator can locate the characters' immediate observations without reading hidden information aloud by accident.
- Any unusual mechanic or trigger can be found in one predictable place during the scene.
- Actor capabilities and the behavior that uses them are close enough to operate without unnecessary page hunting.
- Rewards, clues, exits, and aftermath information are available when the encounter resolves.
- Similar entries follow a stable retrieval order while irrelevant sections are absent rather than blank.
- A facilitator returning to the entry mid-scene can find the needed fact without rereading the whole encounter from the top.

## Notes
Encounter prose has a different retrieval profile from ordinary continuous reading. During live play, the facilitator may move repeatedly among description, hidden state, mechanics, actors, tactics, and aftermath. A predictable structure turns the entry into an interface rather than a miniature short story.

The point is not to impose one universal heading template. The durable operation is separation and colocation: facts that serve different runtime questions should be distinguishable, and facts needed together should be physically close. Consistency helps the facilitator predict where information lives; selective omission keeps the format from becoming padded or bureaucratic.
