# Write a Generative Frame for Unscripted Adventure Scenes

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure gives the operator room to improvise, expand a quiet stretch, or respond to player activity outside the prepared scenes
**THEN** describe the recurring situation well enough to generate new compatible scenes from its actors, rhythms, roles, resources, and pressures, then provide a small number of examples that demonstrate the frame without exhausting it
**ELSE** omit improvisation support when the form deliberately depends on a tightly timed or fully specified sequence

## Do
- Describe what the location, group, or traveling situation repeatedly does over time rather than only what it looks like once.
- Name the characters' standing role in that environment so improvised trouble naturally gives them reasons to become involved.
- Identify recurring people, services, temptations, conflicts, hazards, and authorities that can combine into new scenes.
- Give the environment a usable rhythm such as arrival and departure, day and night, work and rest, public and private, or calm and alert states.
- Supply a few optional encounters as demonstrations of the frame, including why each belongs there and when it is useful.
- Keep improvised material consistent with the scenario's established obligations, power relationships, and available resources.

## Don't
- Call a list of unrelated random events an improvisation framework when it teaches nothing about why those events fit.
- Describe abundant setting color without showing what can happen there or why the characters would care.
- Make every useful service, conflict, or NPC appear only through a prewritten encounter.
- Give examples so prescriptively that the operator cannot infer how to create another scene of the same kind.
- Introduce improvised material that silently changes the characters' authority, mission, or social position.

## Checklist
- The operator can name at least three recurring sources of unscripted scenes without inventing a new premise.
- The characters' role explains why those scenes can involve them.
- The frame includes at least one temporal or situational rhythm that changes what is likely to happen.
- Example encounters illustrate reusable combinations rather than unrelated one-offs.
- A new improvised scene can be checked against existing actors, resources, pressures, and obligations for consistency.
- The framework can expand or compress play without obscuring the main mission.

## Notes
Prepared encounters solve only known moments. A generative frame makes the surrounding situation writable at the table by describing relationships and recurring activity from which further moments can be inferred. A moving community, for example, becomes more useful when the module explains who travels with it, what happens while it moves, what happens when it stops, what the characters are responsible for, what people want, and which kinds of trouble repeatedly arise. Examples then teach the grammar of the situation rather than serving as the only permissible content.

`writing_adventure_modules_variant_make_mobile_key_npc_runnable_through_schedule_and_connections` applies the same generative logic to an important moving person. Give the operator a default routine rather than a guaranteed scene: where the NPC usually goes, which relationships or duties create those movements, when the person is public or isolated, and what circumstances plausibly change the routine. Player action can then intersect the NPC at different times and places without requiring the author to script every meeting in advance.
`writing_adventure_modules_variant_write_wilderness_as_generative_environmental_frame` specializes the frame for outdoor travel. Establish the season and climate, name characteristic terrain, plants, animals, water, visibility, footing, and weather, and identify landscape features that can change route or pressure over time. A small amount of accurate local research gives improvised scenes a shared environmental grammar, so a new crossing, animal sign, storm effect, or travel obstacle feels like another consequence of the same place rather than generic wilderness filler.
