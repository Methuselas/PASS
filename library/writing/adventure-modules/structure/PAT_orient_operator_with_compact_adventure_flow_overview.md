# Orient the Operator with a Compact Adventure Flow Overview

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure spans several sections, locations, or encounters whose relationship must be understood before another operator can run them coherently
**THEN** maintain a working outline while drafting and publish a concise overview that exposes the adventure's major phases, important handoffs, and route toward resolution without duplicating local encounter detail
**ELSE** let a compact scenario move directly from necessary context into its first runnable situation when a separate overview would only repeat the manuscript

## Do
- Keep the working outline current as scenes, clues, actors, and transitions change so later sections do not silently depend on details that were removed or never written.
- Separate prior history from projected play: use background for what happened before the protagonists became involved, and use the overview for the major situations that may arise after play begins.
- Name the major sections or locations, the important actors or discoveries associated with them, and the condition or information that makes the next phase intelligible.
- Describe macro flow at a higher level than the keyed or encounter text; leave checks, statistics, sensory detail, tactics, and local exceptions where they are actually used.
- Recheck the outline near completion for clues, permissions, resources, or other information that later material assumes the players could obtain.
- Let the overview describe alternate or conditional routes when the adventure supports them instead of pretending that one exact sequence is guaranteed.

## Don't
- Assume another operator will reconstruct missing transitions merely because the author knows how the pieces connect.
- Turn the overview into a second full copy of the adventure or a page-by-page summary that must be searched like the body text.
- Present optional events as inevitable simply to make the overview read like a fixed story.
- Leave a critical clue, actor, location, or transition only in the author's private outline when the operator needs it to run later material.
- Preserve an obsolete outline after revision changes the adventure's actual dependencies.

## Checklist
- The working outline and the drafted sections agree about the adventure's major phases and dependencies.
- A reader unfamiliar with the draft can explain the starting situation, major sections, important handoffs, and plausible route toward closure from the overview alone.
- Background information is distinguishable from events that depend on player action after the adventure begins.
- Local rules and encounter detail remain in local sections rather than being duplicated wholesale in the overview.
- Every critical late assumption can be traced to information, access, or state established earlier in the manuscript.
- Conditional or alternate routes are represented as such rather than written as guaranteed protagonist behavior.

## Notes
Adventure authors possess context that never reaches the page unless they deliberately externalize it. A living outline reduces omissions during drafting, while a short operator-facing overview turns that private structural knowledge into a usable map of the whole scenario. The two artifacts do different jobs: one protects continuity while the manuscript changes, and the other lets someone approaching the finished module understand how its large pieces relate before descending into local detail.
