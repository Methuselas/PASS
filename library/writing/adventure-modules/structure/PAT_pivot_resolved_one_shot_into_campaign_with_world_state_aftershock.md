# Pivot a Resolved One-Shot into a Campaign with a World-State Aftershock

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a self-contained adventure may also serve as the opening of a longer campaign
**THEN** resolve the local mission and its immediate consequences first, then reveal a credible larger-world change that creates several concrete continuation vectors without retroactively erasing the meaning of the completed scenario
**ELSE** end with standalone aftermath when no larger transition is needed

## Do
- Give the one-shot a real local stopping condition that can be reached before the larger campaign problem becomes the new active objective.
- Resolve the immediate consequences of the completed mission before introducing the wider change, including losses, relationships, evidence, obligations, or resources that should carry forward.
- Reveal the larger event through information the surviving characters can plausibly receive at that moment, allowing uncertainty when their access is incomplete.
- Make the aftershock large enough to change priorities, routes, loyalties, safety, or available institutions rather than merely adding another ordinary hook.
- Offer several concrete continuation vectors that follow from the characters' current role, location, resources, affiliations, or known opportunities.
- Preserve room for the group to refuse the offered directions, flee, regroup, or pursue another credible response to the changed world.
- If the scenario is run as a true standalone, let the local resolution remain satisfying without requiring the campaign handoff to explain why the adventure mattered.

## Don't
- Interrupt an unresolved local climax with a larger event and then pretend the current mission no longer needs an ending.
- Use the aftershock to reveal that every earlier decision was irrelevant because the old situation has been completely overwritten.
- Introduce a world-changing event with no grounding in the setting merely to manufacture a sequel.
- Offer several campaign labels that all begin from the same situation and differ only in name.
- Require the characters to choose a continuation before they have enough information to understand what changed.
- Treat a campaign handoff as mandatory when the scenario was promised and played as a self-contained one-shot.

## Checklist
- The local adventure has a clear completion state independent of the campaign transition.
- Immediate consequences of the local mission remain true after the larger event appears.
- The characters receive the aftershock through a credible information channel available at the ending.
- The world-state change materially alters at least one priority, route, institution, threat, or opportunity.
- At least two continuation vectors lead to materially different campaign situations.
- The listed continuations follow from established roles, locations, resources, or setting conditions rather than arbitrary sequel premises.
- The module can still stop after the local resolution if no campaign continuation is wanted.

## Notes
A one-shot can close one scale of story while opening another. The key is order: finish the immediate mission, preserve what the characters accomplished or damaged, and only then let a larger external event change the horizon. A partial transmission, emergency broadcast, sudden institutional collapse, public disaster, political upheaval, or other established world event can transform what the survivors need to do next without making the completed scenario feel like disposable prologue.

The continuation section should be actionable rather than promotional. Give the operator distinct directions that begin from the characters' actual end state and explain enough of each route to start the next phase. The larger event creates new choices; it does not revoke the closure the one-shot already earned.
