# Split an Apparent Crisis into Distinct Causal Threads

## Pattern Rule
**IF** an adventure presents several symptoms that characters could reasonably mistake for one problem but the scenario becomes richer when those symptoms have distinct causes, sustaining conditions, or resolution requirements
**THEN** map each causal thread separately with its own manifestations, evidence, reveal, and resolution, then show any shared trigger or overlap so discovering or solving one does not silently resolve the others
**ELSE** keep a single cause when multiplying explanations would add bookkeeping without changing investigation, choice, or consequence

## Do
- Write the operator-facing causal map before writing clues: for each thread, state what caused it, what visible symptoms it produces, what sustains it, and what would actually end or change it.
- Give the shared opening enough overlap that characters can plausibly treat the symptoms as one crisis without making the operator uncertain about the distinction.
- Seed evidence that can progressively separate the threads, such as incompatible timing, different victim patterns, independent witnesses, distinct physical traces, or one symptom persisting after another has been addressed.
- Give each thread at least one reliable route to its own explanation when that explanation matters to completion.
- Let the threads interact where the fiction supports it, but preserve the difference between correlation, common timing, and shared cause.
- State what happens if characters solve one thread while leaving another unresolved.
- When the final truth becomes known, update the practical objective so the operator can distinguish remaining physical, social, supernatural, political, or investigative work.

## Don't
- Use multiple causes only to manufacture a surprise with no earlier evidence that the apparent single explanation was incomplete.
- Make one successful fight, test, or revelation automatically settle every thread when the module established independent causes.
- Scatter the causal explanation so widely that the operator cannot answer a direct question about what is actually happening.
- Add secondary causes that neither change player decisions nor create a distinct resolution condition.
- Confuse simultaneous events with a causal relationship merely because they began at roughly the same time.

## Checklist
- The operator can draw each major symptom to its actual cause without ambiguity.
- At least two threads produce distinguishable evidence before the final explanation.
- Each meaningful thread has its own reveal route and resolution condition.
- Solving one thread leaves a stated, runnable result for any others still active.
- The shared opening remains compatible with the later causal separation.
- The final objective reflects which causes have actually been addressed rather than treating the first solved problem as total completion.

## Notes
A useful multi-threaded mystery can begin with one apparent crisis and later reveal that its symptoms only partly share a cause or trigger. A haunting may coincide with a physical outbreak; sabotage may mask an unrelated personal crime; political unrest may create cover for a separate theft. The technique earns its complexity when separating the causes changes what the characters must learn or do.

Write the causal model for the operator as plainly as possible. Mystery belongs in the players' information state, not in the document's understanding of its own scenario. The operator should always know which evidence belongs to which thread and what remains unresolved after a partial success.
