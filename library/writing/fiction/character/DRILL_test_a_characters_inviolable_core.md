# Test a Character's Claimed Inviolable Core

## Practice Task
Design and draft a short pressure sequence in which a character has already named or demonstrated something they believe cannot be surrendered, then make the climax test that exact value through a vulnerability specific to the established person.

## Target Skill
Designing climax pressure that tests a previously established character value with an individualized vulnerability.

## Setup
Choose a character with a legible loyalty, relationship, moral limit, belief, identity, promise, or self-concept. Write one sentence stating what the character currently believes they would never surrender. Then identify one vulnerability already plausible from the character's body, history, attachment, fear, need, dependence, or environment. Do not invent the vulnerability only because the climax needs a stronger threat.

## Instructions
1. Establish the claimed core before the climax through conduct, dialogue, choice, or recurring pressure. The reader should know what the character believes is non-negotiable without needing a last-minute explanatory speech.
2. Establish the vulnerability independently. Give it at least one earlier appearance or consequence so it belongs to the character rather than to the climax machinery.
3. Build an escalation ladder that attacks easier layers first: convenience, reputation, comfort, possessions, status, safety, outward compliance, or another value the character can surrender without yet giving up the core claim.
4. At each step, record what the character gives up and what remains intact. Do not treat confession, obedience, fear, or pain as proof that the deepest value has already changed unless the story has demonstrated that connection.
5. Make the final pressure connect the established vulnerability directly to the claimed core. The climax should force a real choice, internal fracture, substitution, reinterpretation, or refusal rather than merely increase generic danger.
6. Draft the outcome without predetermining moral victory. The character may hold, break, bargain, redirect the cost, discover that the claim was false, or redefine what the value means.
7. Write the immediate aftermath. Show at least one behavioral, relational, bodily, or perceptual consequence that proves what changed and what did not.
8. Run the counterfactual test: substitute a different character with different attachments or fears. If the same climax works unchanged, personalize the pressure further.

## Success Check
- The claimed inviolable value is established before the final test.
- The vulnerability is specific to the character and planted before it becomes decisive.
- Earlier escalation distinguishes outward compliance or lesser losses from the core being tested.
- The climax attacks the exact value the story has prepared rather than applying undifferentiated maximum danger.
- The outcome can credibly include integrity, compromise, fracture, substitution, or transformation; the drill does not assume one morally preferred result.
- The aftermath provides observable evidence of the resulting state.
- Replacing the character would require changing the climactic pressure.

## Common Failures
- Announcing the supposedly deepest value only moments before testing it.
- Inventing a phobia, weakness, or hostage at the climax because ordinary stakes feel insufficient.
- Treating increased physical pain as automatically more character-specific pressure.
- Equating forced speech or temporary compliance with total internal conversion without evidence.
- Rewarding the character with easy moral victory because the writer is afraid to let a sincerely held value fail.
- Breaking the character solely for shock without tracing why this particular pressure reaches this particular core.
- Ending at the forced choice without showing what the outcome changed.

## Notes
The drill trains pressure design, not torture scenes. The same structure can apply to loyalty under social exclusion, professional ethics under career threat, addiction under access to relief, family obligation under scarcity, identity under assimilation pressure, grief under a demand to move on, or a relationship under a choice between attachment and self-preservation. The useful question is not "what is the worst thing imaginable?" but "what pressure would specifically test the value this character has already claimed as non-negotiable?"
