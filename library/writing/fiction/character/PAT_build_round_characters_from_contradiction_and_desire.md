# Build Round Characters from Contradiction and Desire

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a main character can be reduced to one admirable trait, flaw, or story function
**THEN** give the character a pressing desire, conflicting qualities, and choices whose consequences disclose a coherent but not fully predictable person
**ELSE** keep a single-trait character only when that narrowness performs a limited supporting function

## Do
- Define what the character wants badly enough to risk trouble, make a poor decision, or injure another priority.
- Separate the desire the character names from the need their repeated choices protect. When the character lacks self-knowledge, make the operative desire legible through pursuit, avoidance, or sacrifice rather than requiring a confession.
- Pair an obvious quality with a countervailing impulse: generosity with vanity, competence with avoidance, loyalty with envy, or another tension the story can activate.
- Let pressure force choices between values so complexity appears in action rather than in an inventory of traits.
- At the threshold of success, test whether attainment threatens another need, removes a sustaining excuse or identity, or exposes the stated goal as a proxy; prepare that pressure before the reversal.
- Make surprising behavior grow from motives and conditions already available to the reader, even when the connection becomes clear only afterward.

## Don't
- Make goodness, wickedness, beauty, eccentricity, or victimhood the whole personality of a central character.
- Protect the protagonist from bad judgment merely to keep the reader's approval.
- Add random inconsistency and call it complexity.
- Call self-sabotage or disappointment psychologically deep when no competing motive or prior evidence makes the response intelligible.
- Give every minor role a full psychological arc when a precise functional presence is all the story needs.

## Checklist
- The central desire can generate action and trouble rather than merely decorate a biography.
- If the character misnames or cannot recognize the operative desire, repeated behavior gives the reader enough evidence to infer it.
- At least two qualities can pull the character toward incompatible choices.
- A consequential decision reveals more than a descriptive label could.
- Any self-blocked attainment or post-attainment disillusionment traces to pressure established before the turn.
- A surprising action remains convincing when traced back through motive and pressure.
- Any flat supporting character throws a central character or conflict into sharper relief.

## Notes
Roundness is not the number of facts known about a person. It is the sense that desire, defect, virtue, and pressure can combine in more than one plausible way. Fiction often becomes active when a person wants something enough to choose badly and must then live inside the result.

Desire does not have to be conscious or accurately named by the character, but it must be designed clearly enough that conduct supplies the missing knowledge. Apparently anti-goal behavior need not be random: nearing success can threaten belonging, safety, identity, or the excuse that has organized a life, and attaining a proxy goal can expose the unmet need beneath it. Preparing that conflict preserves surprise without sacrificing coherence.

`writing_fiction_variant_build_compromised_character_from_regret_capability_and_code` enters through consequential history rather than present desire alone. Choose one past mistake, loss, or failure that the character regrets or still pays for; trace at least one present capability to the life that produced it; name one liability, relationship, debt, fear, or temptation that can bring that history back into current action; and define the value, obligation, or personal code the character still protects when easier choices would violate it. A vice, coping habit, attraction, or avoidance earns space only when it can alter a present choice or relationship. The past should create live pressure, not explain every trait or guarantee redemption.
