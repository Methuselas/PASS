# Build Plot from Causal Consequences

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a fiction draft presents events in sequence but leaves the reader asking only what happened next
**THEN** connect major events through motive, choice, pressure, and consequence so each change creates or constrains what can follow
**ELSE** retain chronological adjacency only where the relation itself is the intended mystery or effect

## Do
- For each major event, write both what changed and why that change occurred at this point rather than elsewhere in the chronology.
- Make a character's response alter the next available choice instead of restoring the same conditions after every scene.
- Carry consequences forward even when the narrative skips time; a transition can compress duration without resetting causality.
- Use coincidence to create trouble more readily than to solve trouble that character action has made difficult.
- When a setback is meant to carry dramatic weight, derive it from an established choice, delay, antagonist response, vulnerability, or other condition the reader can trace; use pure bad luck sparingly unless chance itself is part of the governing pressure.

## Don't
- Treat temporal order as proof that one event caused another.
- Add complications that occupy pages but leave motives, options, and stakes unchanged.
- Present arbitrary misfortune as though it were an earned reversal when no prior action, pressure, or established condition bears on the outcome.
- Explain causality after the fact when scene action could let readers infer it.
- Resolve a chain through an event that neither grows from prior pressure nor changes its meaning.

## Checklist
- Every major turn has an identifiable cause inside the story's established conditions.
- Consequences narrow, widen, or transform later choices.
- Removing an event would break a specific causal link rather than merely shorten elapsed time.
- Major setbacks meant to feel earned can be traced to prior choice, pressure, opposition, vulnerability, or another established condition rather than arbitrary misfortune alone.
- When public speech is meant to matter, removing the earlier words weakens a later choice, judgment, obligation, or consequence.
- The climax results from accumulated action and pressure rather than arriving as an unrelated episode.
- The reader can ask both what happens next and why it must happen this way.

## Notes
A story records events; plot makes their relations consequential. The difference is not that every cause must be announced. Readers can infer motive and pressure from action, but the writer must know how one scene changes the conditions of the next. Causal design turns a string of incidents into an accumulating structure.

`writing_fiction_variant_make_interpretation_causally_operative` adds interpretation as an explicit causal step. Map the chain as evidence -> interpretation or model -> choice -> consequence -> new evidence. The wrong reading must be plausible from the character's available information, and a corrected reading must change what they would do; otherwise the interpretation is characterization rather than plot. Hidden relationship state, unequal knowledge, and model lag can all create divergence, but the route stays fair only when the reader can trace why each person formed the model they used.

`writing_fiction_variant_make_worldview_predict_behavior` makes an idea operational. Translate the belief into at least one prediction about what the character thinks they can, must, or will do; put that prediction under real story pressure; then let success, failure, or contradiction create a consequence. Another character may also use the stated worldview as evidence when interpreting later conduct. Keep the idea from becoming a detachable speech: if removing it leaves motive, inference, and consequence unchanged, it has not become part of the plot.

`writing_fiction_variant_make_public_speech_causally_binding` treats speech as a state change rather than detachable characterization. Identify what the words create in the social world—reputation, debt, permission, expectation, warning, or a standard against which conduct will be judged—then build at least one later moment that has to answer to that changed state. The later action may keep, revise, exploit, or betray the words, but deleting the earlier speech should reduce causal or social pressure rather than merely remove a memorable line.

`writing_fiction_variant_control_the_evidence_ecology` moves one step upstream from interpretation. Map who can create, access, preserve, destroy, compare, corroborate, classify, or name the evidence on which later models depend. Suppression becomes causal only when it changes a character's available inference or choice; do not confuse authorial concealment from the reader with in-world control of verification.
