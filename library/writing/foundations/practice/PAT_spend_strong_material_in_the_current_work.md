# Spend Strong Material in the Current Work

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a strong scene, image, line, reveal, structural move, or other piece of material genuinely fits the current work and is being withheld mainly from fear that a later section or future project will otherwise lack something equally good
**THEN** use the fitting material where it can strengthen the current work, and defer it only when later placement has a specific structural, informational, tonal, or causal function
**ELSE** preserve the material for later when it does not fit the present work or when its effect depends on conditions the current draft has not established.

## Do
- Compare weak placeholder material against the stronger fitting idea instead of protecting the stronger option merely because it feels scarce.
- Ask why an idea is being saved. Keep the delay when setup, contrast, suspense, escalation, character change, information control, or another identifiable craft function makes later placement stronger.
- Use the best current-fit material rather than assuming a future work is more deserving simply because it is imagined as larger or more important.
- Preserve ideas that do not belong in the current work in a retrievable form; using strong material now does not mean forcing every good idea into one draft.
- Let current work earn attention through what is actually on the page rather than through promises that the best material will arrive much later.
- Trust continued invention enough to solve future needs when they become real writing problems instead of padding the current work to protect a hypothetical reserve.

## Don't
- Keep knowingly weaker material solely because the stronger alternative is being saved for later.
- Spend every attractive idea at once without regard for coherence, emphasis, pacing, or the work's actual scope.
- Confuse "strong" with loud, shocking, elaborate, or maximal; the strongest material is the material that best performs the present craft job.
- Reveal a mystery, climax, emotional turn, or structural payoff early when its later position is what gives it force.
- Cannibalize material that clearly belongs to another project just to satisfy a rule against saving ideas.

## Checklist
- Any strong material left unused has a reason stronger than fear of running out of ideas.
- The current draft is not carrying filler whose main purpose is to reserve better material for an imagined future.
- Delayed material can name the setup, contrast, information state, tonal progression, or causal dependency that makes delay useful.
- The selected strong material fits the current work's purpose rather than merely displaying invention.
- Preserved unused ideas remain available without controlling the quality of the work being written now.

## Notes
Creative scarcity can make a writer treat a strong idea as a nonrenewable resource: protect it for the perfect future chapter, project, or audience, and let the current work survive on safer material. That protection can become self-defeating when the future work never arrives or when the present work is weakened to preserve it. The corrective is not indiscriminate front-loading. It is to distinguish **functional delay** from **scarcity-driven hoarding**.

Variant `writing_variant_keep_introductory_adventure_distinctive_while_simplifying_entry` applies the same decision to first exposure. Newcomers may need clearer instructions, fewer dependencies, or a bounded scope, but those usability constraints do not by themselves require bland or unrepresentative material. Preserve the form's characteristic promise inside the part newcomers can actually use.
