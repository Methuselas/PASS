# Synthesize Literary Influences into Original Voice

## Pattern Rule
**IF** fear of influence is limiting a writer's reading or the emerging voice relies too heavily on one admired author
**THEN** study widely within the intended genre, test admired techniques deliberately, and combine lessons from multiple writers into a distinct style
**ELSE** keep reading across periods and current venues to refresh the available craft repertoire

## Do
- Read for particular capabilities: line and meter, plot construction, comedy, sentence movement, or another feature the work needs.
- When style is the capability, inspect the repeated joints and habits that make the prose move: conjunctions, clause hierarchy, sentence openings, punctuation, paragraph turns, diction, tonal pivots, and characteristic departures from smooth default syntax.
- Attempt a close imitation as an experiment, then notice which borrowed choices reveal your own abilities and inclinations.
- Mix incompatible or distant influences and retain only the parts that become useful in the new work.
- Treat explicit cards and memories as diagnostic and training scaffolds, not as the outer boundary of the writer's available repertoire; compose from the full available craft repertoire, then use canon and memory to inspect and refine choices.

## Don't
- Protect originality by avoiding predecessors and contemporary writers in the genre.
- Confuse productive influence with copying another writer's phrases, mannerisms, or surface tricks unchanged.

## Checklist
- The writer can name what each major influence taught rather than merely listing admired authors.
- The resulting work transforms its precedents instead of remaining recognizable as a single-author imitation.
- Reading includes both established work and examples of what the genre is doing now.

## Notes
Originality is made through absorption and self-discovery rather than received as an untouched gift. Deep reading shows what a genre has already accomplished, supplies proven craft options, and reveals the writer's own tendencies. A personal style emerges as influences are selected, combined, outgrown, and redirected.


`writing_variant_reverse_engineer_microstyle_before_synthesis` treats style as a field of repeated local decisions rather than an adjective attached to an author. A writer may favor coordination over subordination, return often to `and` or `but`, delay the main clause, interrupt with dashes, close paragraphs on blunt statements, shift registers abruptly, or tolerate a roughness that a generic revision pass would normalize. The study is useful only when the choice is tied to an effect and then transformed. A brief close imitation can expose the mechanics, but the final test moves to unrelated material and combines influences until the result is no longer a single-author pastiche.


`writing_variant_preserve_deep_style_while_adapting_surface_to_form` prevents two opposite errors: flattening every subcategory into one signature surface, and assuming that adaptation to genre requires abandoning the writer's deeper preferences. A writer may repeatedly favor causal architecture, concrete objects, moral ambiguity, compressed images, or particular cadence while expressing those preferences very differently in a poem, a first-person story, an analytical essay, or a technical explanation. A recurring trait counts as style only when it remains a useful choice; repeated defects should be corrected rather than protected as identity.
