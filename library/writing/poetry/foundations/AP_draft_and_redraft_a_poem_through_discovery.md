# Draft and Redraft a Poem Through Discovery

## Objective
Develop a charged stimulus into a self-supporting poem through bounded generation, evidence-based successive drafts, recoverable choices, incubation, and a stopping decision grounded in the work rather than in fatigue or attachment.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Choose live pressure.** Begin with an image, object, phrase, memory, action, quotation, or question that produces sensory, verbal, emotional, or intellectual energy. If the material comes from another work, activate `PAT_transform_source_material_into_a_standalone_poem` and establish the attribution and reuse constraints before drafting. **Advance gate:** the stimulus creates something to notice or pursue, not merely a broad topic to explain.
2. **Generate without requiring a destination.** Activate `PAT_draft_poems_through_discovery`. Write continuously for a bounded interval, following particular words, rhythms, associations, and turns. Let live material depart from the prompt rather than forcing every line to demonstrate compliance. **Recovery:** if nothing gains pressure, change the stimulus or starting condition and run another bounded pass.
3. **Read the page as evidence.** After the interval, reread the actual language rather than the intended poem. Mark the phrases, images, sounds, contradictions, or movements that remain alive; also mark uncertainty and missing work. Name one working center and one next action. **Advance gate:** at least one marked element can organize or provoke another pass.
4. **Make a poem-shaped draft.** Compose beyond the generative notes. Choose a working sequence, voice, scale, and page shape that allow the emerging center to develop, but do not treat early lineation or form as irrevocable. Cut prompt language that has finished its generative job. **Advance gate:** the draft has an intelligible movement or pressure that a later pass can test as a whole.
5. **Preserve, diagnose, and redraft.** Activate `PAT_separate_generative_and_evaluative_writing_modes` before consequential changes, then activate `PAT_revise_whole_draft_toward_intended_form`. Save a recoverable state, name the largest mismatch, and make a pass that cuts, expands, reorders, or reconceives in response. Move even vivid details when a different position better serves the poem's movement. Compare states and restore earlier material only when it now performs a necessary job.
6. **Use distance when attention narrows.** If changes merely reverse one another or become local fiddling, activate `PAT_reassess_inspired_drafts_after_distance`. Rest the poem or use the rotation variant in `DRILL_write_cull_and_rework_poems_to_train_judgment` to advance another viable draft before returning with a named question. **Recovery:** when repeated distance produces no new consequential diagnosis, move to the completion gate instead of manufacturing changes.
7. **Test the reader's frame.** Activate `PAT_use_a_poem_title_as_an_active_frame` to test what the title makes a reader expect and how it changes after the ending. When an outside text or unfamiliar context remains necessary, activate `PAT_add_paratext_only_when_a_poem_needs_external_framing`; otherwise remove the borrowed scaffold and let the poem carry its own situation.
8. **Close deliberately.** Read the complete poem for movement, language, sound, page shape, and the relation between opening, turns, and ending. Stop when no named consequential mismatch remains, the title and any external frame perform necessary work, borrowed material is properly handled, and another pass would only exchange defensible alternatives without improving the governing effect.

## Notes
Successive drafting is not a march from bad wording to polished wording. Early notes may contain only the argument's hinge, a sensory action, or an image system; later passes discover what those fragments can organize. A useful genealogy keeps prior choices recoverable without turning every discarded line into an obligation. Incubation is active when it restores judgment, and completion is active when the writer can name why the surviving structure, details, and frame belong.
